  <p>February 28th, 2014</p>
  <p><strong>Wren</strong></p>
</blockquote>

<p>It hurt in the same way it always did when the door opened and light streamed into Wren’s face. The sudden transition from pitch black darkness to the bright light from outside the closet always made her cringe away from it. It blinded her, and she was left helpless for a moment as her eyes struggled to see so that she could determine who was on the other side. It could be <em>him</em>, or it could be Dominic. There was no way to tell in those first moments.</p>

<p>“Oh, little dove.”</p>

<p>The voice made the tension immediately leave Wren’s body, and she blinked her green eyes into focus just as Dominic knelt down in front of her. She was curled into a ball. It was the same way he found her every time he opened the door to the damn closet. She was always curled up with blood coating her arms from scratches she gave herself trying to feel <em>something</em>. Long ago, she gave up trying to claw at the wooden door. It wouldn’t open, not until they wanted it to. Not until she was driven absolutely mad.</p>

<p>“D-D…”</p>

<p>“It’s okay.”</p>

<p>His arms went around her, and she felt herself be lifted into his arms. He didn’t seem to mind the fact that she was getting blood on his white button up shirt or how much of a mess she was. He was her guardian angel, and one day he would fulfill the promise to whisk her from the Manor to safety. One day, he would save her just like he said. Until then, she had to be strong in a way that was becoming more and more difficult with each passing day.</p>

<p>She felt the rock of his hips as he carried her out of the basement. She closed her eyes and pressed her face against his chest. She remembered why she was down in the basement and why she was in the closet. She’d been too bold. She’d asked for too much. She had thought, for one stupid, foolish moment, that she could appeal to August’s humanity. She had been so, so wrong. She always was.</p>

<p><em>“I don’t want to hear you ask again, Wren,”</em> He snapped as he turned to look at her. She cowered at the sight of his cruel, cold eyes. <em>“Art is a hobby. It’s something for little girls to entertain themselves with and, if we decide, something for you to show off when it suits us. It’s not something to be studied as if it actually matters.”</em></p>

<p><em>“I’m sor-”</em> She began, but she was cut off by him crossing the room. Foolishly, she took a step back.</p>

<p><em>“You know better than to pull away from me.”</em></p>

<p>His fingers caught around her wrist and yanked her forward. She stumbled against his chest. Emerald eyes lifted to catch his impossibly cold blue ones. She opened her mouth to stammer out another apology, but his free hand came to snatch the sketchbook she had attempted to present to him only moments before from her grasp. She had thought, foolishly, that he might see her talent for the hobby. Wren watched in horror as he tossed it into the fireplace with a simple flick of his wrist.</p>

<p><em>“Augus-”</em></p>

<p><em>“Besides,”</em> August stated, his lip turning up in a smirk. He enjoyed this. He always had. <em>“It’s not as if you have any talent for it.”</em></p>

<p>He gripped her wrist and pulled her back against his chest as he turned her to watch the fireplace. She didn’t try to fight him, though his grip remained impossibly tight on both of her slender wrists. He held her there for what felt like forever as she watched her most prized possession, her <em>only</em> possession, turn to ash.</p>

<p><em>“Remember, Wren. You are nothing.”</em></p>

<p>Wren had known in that moment exactly what would happen next. She tried, foolishly, to fight him as he led her down the stairs to the basement. He shoved her down the last few stairs. She stumbled and pain had flared in her ankle. He didn’t give her time to pick herself up. He gripped her elbow and hoisted her to her feet. Then, he shoved her in the closet and slammed the door shut to leave Wren in the pitch black hell.</p>

<p>“Little dove, are you alright?”</p>

<p>Dominic’s voice pulled her from her thoughts. They were in his room, and she wanted to cry simply from the sight. She was so sure he’d take her back to the room that was, technically, her own. The yellow wallpaper terrified her in the way it seemed to move on its own. She knew, surely, that she was losing her mind day by day.</p>

<p>“I-”</p>

<p>“Here,” He murmured. He stroked her hair for a second before a bottle of water was pressed into her hand. “You must be thirsty.”</p>

<p>She was. She could barely lift it to her lips. Her body ached with exhaustion, but she forced her arms to work. She lifted the bottle to her lips and took a long drink. When the liquid hit her stomach, Wren wanted to retch. She was hungry. How long had she been down there?</p>

<p>“Two days,” Dominic sighed as if he could read her mind. He reached to the tray she hadn’t noticed before and handed her a piece of bread. “I tried to get him to let me get you sooner. I’m so sorry.”</p>

<p>“It’s not your fault,” She managed finally. She tore tiny pieces of the bread to put in her mouth. She wanted more, but they both knew that her stomach couldn’t handle anything larger. Not after two days in the basement.</p>

<p>“I should protect you.”</p>

<p>“You <em>do</em> protect me.”</p>

<p>Wren looked up to him and saw the tender way he looked at her. In the seven years he had been in her life, she had marveled at him. He was the most beautiful, incredible thing she had ever seen. She was seven when he first appeared in her life. Dominic was weeks away from seventeen and, like her, an orphan after the tragic death of his parents. He was the first soul she had seen other than her brother in the time she lived at the Manor. He showed her kindness, maybe even love. It was no wonder she considered him her Prince Charming. As they read fairy tale after fairy tale together, Dominic soon became the prince of all of her tales with his dark hair and blue eyes. Each time he pulled her to safety from her brother’s cruelty, she fell a little further.</p>

<p>“Let me.”</p>

<p>He didn’t have to elaborate. Obediently, she held her arms out to him. He examined the damage she’d done. She watched him reach into his pocket to pull out the small vial. A healing tonic. She knew it well. When he opened it and held it to her lips, she swallowed the bitter liquid without complaint. It didn’t take more than a few seconds for the cuts on her arms to stitch together. Soon, all that was left was the blood that caked her arms.</p>

<p>“Thank you.”</p>

<p>“Of course, little dove,” Dominic murmured. He tucked a lock of her blonde hair behind her ear. “When you’re steady, go shower. You can use my bathroom. I set out one of my shirts for you, and a pair of my boxers.”</p>

<p>“And th-”</p>

<p>“And then,” He continued, already knowing her question. He cupped her face and leaned forward to press a kiss to her forehead. Her green eyes fluttered closed and she let herself take in his comfort. “You can sleep here. I’ll keep you safe, my darling.”</p>

<p>It was all the incentive she needed. Wren lifted herself off of his bed. Each step was unsteady, but she didn’t ask for help. She glanced back to him with a smile just before she ducked into the bathroom. Immediately, she turned on the water in the shower. Her eyes were downcast as she removed her bloody clothing and carefully knelt to place it in the bin.</p>

<p>As she stood, she caught sight of herself in the full length mirror. At fourteen, her body was still gangly and thin from childhood, and a little bit of starvation, yet slowly starting to fill out with slight curves. The other day, when she joined him for dinner on his birthday, Dominic called her beautiful. Her pale skin was coated in bruises and blood from cuts the tonic healed. Her hair, long and blonde, stuck to her head in matted clumps from her sweat. Hollow green eyes stared back at her. She didn’t see beautiful like he did, but she <em>wanted</em> to be the girl he saw.</p>

<p>She tried not to take much time in the shower. As the conditioner soaked in her hair, she scrubbed her body until all the blood was gone. Her skin was raw from the combination of the scalding water and the rag she used on her skin. When she finished, she wrapped herself in his towel. Her hair was combed out and twisted into pigtail braids, her teeth brushed, and his clothes were pulled onto her body. His shirt hit her just below the knees. A dress for her. In his clothes, she looked even smaller.</p>

<p>“Sorry I took so long,” She murmured when she returned to the room.</p>

<p>“You deserved a nice shower.”</p>

<p>In her absence, he lit a fire. The result was not only the warmth that Wren desperately craved, but a soft light cast over the room. The small gesture provided an endless amount of comfort for the young girl. When he held his hand out to her, she went to him without question.</p>

<p>“You look like you feel better,” He murmured as he wrapped her in his arms.</p>

<p>“I do. Thank you. For everything.”</p>

<p>Her own arms went around his middle. She was short compared to his height. She barely came to his midsection, but she held him all the same. She turned her head and took in his scent. She couldn’t identify the scent of him, but he smelled like outside. He smelled like all the things Wren wanted in her life.</p>

<p>“I have a present for you, little dove.”</p>

<p>“For me?”</p>

<p>He pulled back and took her hand. She let herself be pulled over to the bed and pressed to sit on it. He tucked his thick blanket over her lap before a small box was handed to her. She held it in her hands, sure that it couldn’t possibly be for her. Presents weren’t typical in her life.</p>

<p>“Yes, it’s for you. Open it, darling.”</p>

<p>Wren smiled up at him before she carefully removed the ribbon that tied the box shut. She was careful and meticulous with the packaging. Luckily, he didn’t seem to mind. He watched her as she lifted the lid from the box to reveal a leather bound sketch book. Immediately, her eyes lifted to meet his. Her lips parted, but she found no words.</p>

<p>“He told me what he did. I called him cruel for it. I thought you deserved a new one. Open it.”</p>

<p>Wren undid the buttons that held it shut and opened the cover. Her fingers ran over the soft, luxurious pages of the sketchbook. The paper felt impossibly smoothe under her fingertips. On the back of the cover, the pouch held a set of colored pencils. Her first set. Until then, her sketches were with pencils or found ballpoint pens.</p>

<p>“I… Dominic…”</p>

<p>“Do you like it?”</p>

<p>“I <em>love</em> it, Dominic. I love <em>you</em>.”</p>

<p>As the words left her lips, Wren’s body tensed with anxiety. She had known it for years. Possibly since the very first time he sat beside her with his kind, sweet smile. She loved him with everything she had, but he could never give it to her in return. Who could love her?</p>

<p>“I’m so sorry I-”</p>

<p>“Me too.”</p>

<p>Slowly, he took the box out of her hand. Her eyes followed the sketchbook as he set it on the nightstand. He moved to sit closer to her with his back against the headboard. He motioned her to him, and she instantly crawled into his arms. She had done it countless times before, but it was <em>different</em> now. This time, he wasn’t seeing her as a child, but something else entirely.</p>

<p>His hand came to rest on the side of her chin. When he guided her gaze to his, she obeyed without a second thought. His light blue eyes met her own. In them, she saw all the kindness she was sure she didn’t deserve. In his smile, she found love she craved. When he leaned in, she did, too. When his lips met her own, explosions erupted in her chest.</p>

<p>Their kiss, her very first, began with soft brushes of lips. Each movement made her shiver with delight. She melted against him. His hand slid to the small of her back as his tongue brushed along her lower lip. Her mouth parted and a soft whimper left her at the way their tongues pressed together. He pulled her closer. The kiss deepened in intensity. She was out of breath when he guided her to lay on her back. He straddled her as he leaned over her.</p>

<p>When they broke, he was smiling.</p>

<p>Wren would do <em>anything</em> to keep him smiling at her like that.</p>

<p>“You love me, little dove?” His voice was a hopeful whisper. Wren reached up to let her fingers trail along his cheek. When he leaned into her touch, she felt her heart fill with warmth.</p>

<p>“I do. I love you, Dominic. I love you more than anything.”</p>

<p>His smile grew. He leaned down to kiss her once more. Again, their bodies pressed together and he shifted to press some of his weight on her. She felt something press against her leg. It took her a moment before she realized it was <em>him.</em> His fingers brushed along the inner skin of her thigh. The heat spread through her like wildfire.</p>

<p>“I think it’s time you show me just how much,” He whispered. He pulled his lips off hers to meet her eyes. He smiled at her. There was something else in his eyes: a need she had never seen. A need <em>she</em> wanted to fulfill. “You want that, don’t you?”</p>

<p>“Yes,” Wren whispered. “Yes. Anything you want.”</p>

<p>“Good.”</p>

<p>His smile turned to a smirk as fingers hooked in the boxers she was wearing. She felt them slide down her legs as their eyes remained locked. She had no idea what would come next, but if he kept smiling at her, she would be anything she asked.</p>

<p>“Good girl, little dove.”</p>

<p>“I love you, Dominic,” She murmured as his fingers trailed along her skin.</p>

<p>“I know, Wren,” He said softly. “I know you do.”</p>
