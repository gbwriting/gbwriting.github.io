<blockquote class="quote-alt"><p>April 23rd, 2016</p><p><strong>Hendrix</strong></p></blockquote><p>Hendrix thumbed through the program in his hands as he leaned against the wall of the packed theater where New York University’s dance program was holding their spring showcase. As always, his eyes were searching for one name, and one name only. When he saw her listed under other groups of contemporary performers, the Fae grinned.</p><p>“What are <em>you</em> doing here?”</p><p>He didn’t have to look up to know the voice, and he reached to pinch the bridge of his nose as he withheld his gaze from him. When it came to dealing with the man who had approached him, Hendrix found a greater than average need for deep breaths. In his long life, no one had worn on his patience quite as much as Ezra James. Still, Hendrix managed to put a smile on his face as he turned to look at the Fae beside him.</p><p>“I would argue that’s quite obvious, isn’t it?” Hendrix held up the program. “I’m watching a performance.”</p><p>“You’re not meant to let her see you.”</p><p>“I’m well aware of that.”</p><p>“And what are you-”</p><p>“Ezra, if Amelia can see me standing in the very, <em>very</em> back of a theater full of hundreds of people with stage lights in her face, then we have a girl with <em>magnificent </em>vision. We’re fine.”</p><p>“You have orders.”</p><p>It took everything within the Fae to not roll his eyes at Ezra’s words. Somehow, he kept the patient, practiced smile on his face. He knew how to keep his composure in moments where people bothered him, though it was <em>often</em> a struggle when he spoke to Ezra and was constantly reminded of his <em>orders</em>. He knew his orders without the additional prompting. His <em>orders </em>were the very thing he was <em>attempting</em> to carry out.</p><p>Around them, people were turning their heads in an attempt to see what was going on. Ezra’s voice was louder than Hendrix was sure he meant for it to be. People were catching on that there was something happening between the two men. He could have remained invisible in the crowd without Ezra’s interference. Now, that was looking like it wouldn’t be possible.</p><p>“You’re the one causing a scene,” Hendrix replied in a sharp whisper.</p><p>“You need to leave.”</p><p>“Like hell.”</p><p>Hendrix glanced in the direction of the stage. In a matter of minutes, the performances would begin. With any hope, he would be able to watch them in peace. Though, with the way Ezra crossed his arms over his chest and glared at him, he was quickly losing faith in being able to accomplish the quiet mission he had set himself on for the evening.</p><p>“Do I need to contac-” Ezra started. Before he could finish the sentence, Hendrix cut him off.</p><p>“Go ahead. Who do you think <em>gave </em>me the order to be here, Ezra?”</p><p>Over the past fourteen years, there had been many of these conversations. Frankly, Hendrix was tired of them. He had put up with them for a time, but not anymore. He knew what his orders were and what his job was when it came to the girl they were <em>both </em>stationed in New York City to protect. Sometimes, though he didn’t dare voice it, he wondered if Ezra realized his <em>own</em> orders.</p><p>When the decision had been made to send Amelia Aldersley, now James, to the city in an attempt to protect her, her mother, her sisters, and the <em>world </em>from Jo’s visions about Amelia’s future, the original plan was for her to leave with her Protector, Felicity, and one Knight, Ezra. The two of them would pose as her parents and keep her disguised as a human who was blissfully unaware of the supernatural world she was unknowingly at the center of. Ezra and Felicity were the ones meant to protect her. For months leading up to their departure, the plan remained the same.</p><p><em>It was the week before they were scheduled to leave when the Fae Queen, Eilian, called him to her office. As always, he took the meeting obediently. There was no reason not to. At his very core, he was loyal to Eilian and her daughters. He would do anything to protect them just as he would do anything to protect his aunt, the Fae Queen’s longtime partner and soulmate.</em>&nbsp;</p><p><em>“My Queen?” Hendrix called out with a gentle knock to the door frame.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>He had let himself in to the Aldersley Manor as usual. In some ways, it was as much home to the Knight as his own apartment or Jo’s house in the Eastern part of the city. Just like the girls, he had grown up here, too. In many ways, he considered the Queen to be another mother. She wasn’t his biological mother, who he barely remembered other than her warm smile and bright red hair, nor either of his aunts, but Eilian held a place in his heart all the same. He would do anything for her out of love, not just because she was his Queen.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Hendrix,” Eilian murmured as she sat up fully. He hadn’t realized it before, but she had tears streaking down her cheeks. “Come in. Shut the door.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Yes, my Queen.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>He did as he was told and stepped fully into the office. The heavy wooden door was shut behind him. Eyes scanned the room for Jo, who was almost always at the Fae Queen’s side when she was doing anything for the Monarchy. Or, truly, anything else. Jo and Eilian were soulmates in every sense of the word, and it was rare to see one without the other.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Where’s…?”</em></p><p><em>“She’s at home today. I’ll see her in a bit. I… I wanted to talk to you.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Anything, my Queen.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Have a seat.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>Eilian stood and walked over to the couch on the other side of her large office. She sat upon it and motioned to the armchair across from her. Obediently, Hendrix moved to sit down. His posture was straight, just as he had watched others do around the Queen throughout his long life. He couldn’t think of a single time that he had taken a meeting alone with her. He was nervous. Had he done something to upset her?</em></p><p><em>“Hendrix…” Eilian’s voice cracked. “I…”&nbsp;</em></p><p>S<em>he reached up to run her fingers through her long blonde hair. As she did so, Hendrix truly took in the state of his Queen. Her eyes were red and puffy. They leaked tears that stained her cheeks. Clearly, those tears had been plaguing her for some time. She was wearing a pair of pajama pants, a white t-shirt, and a terrycloth robe over that. Her hair was standing in tangled tuffs atop her head. Never, in all the years of his life, had he seen her in such a state. Even in the hardest of times, Eilian was always put together.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“I don’t know if I can do this, Hendrix. I don’t think I can send Amelia to New York.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>He didn’t know what to say. In many ways, he couldn’t blame her for the reluctance. He couldn’t imagine the pain of sending someone she loved, particularly her own child, away to be raised by someone else. It was no secret, especially not to him, that Jackson didn’t agree with the plan. The Hunter had made his objections to the situation well known in every meeting and conversation. Jackson made sure they all knew that he thought Eilian to be a terrible mother for the decisions that would soon be carried out.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>What Jackson didn’t understand, or couldn’t understand, was that Eilian never had the good fortune of thinking only of herself and her children. She had to think about the entire world and every life that existed within it. Unfortunately, the prophesy was clear: Amelia, if left in Greenborough, would destroy the world.</em></p><p><em>It was something Hendrix still struggled with believing.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>He still failed to see how the sweet child they all knew and loved could be destined for what Jo had seen in her future. He knew his aunt and trusted her completely. If she had seen it, the vision was true. Even with that undying trust for the woman who raised him, the visions she continued to see didn’t match up to the Amelia Aldersley he knew.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>He had only gotten a quick moment with her as he entered the Manor for his meeting with the Queen, but in their small interaction, the child once again proved her sweetness. She was in the living room with Saoirse, Percy, Piper, and Riley making Valentine’s Day cards. He had come into the house just in time to watch her press a pink, heart shaped sticker onto Piper’s forehead. The baby had instantly erupted into a bright smile. Amelia noticed him then and had proudly given him a bright green heart for his shirt. Green, she had told him, like his eyes. He wore it proudly.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“My Queen…” Hendrix murmured, unsure what to do to comfort Eilian. He wished, just as everyone did, he had the power to reassure her that it wasn’t necessary to send Amelia away. The truth remained that they simply had no way of knowing it would be okay if they didn’t.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Will you go with her?”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“I… what?”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>The request stunned him, and he looked up to meet her eyes. They were still filled with tears. In the darkened blue irises, he saw every bit of her pain. He would do anything to take it away. It couldn’t be, not fully, and the thought killed him.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Will you go with her to New York? Will you protect her, too?”</em></p><p><em>“Yes-” He began, but before he could get more than the beginnings of his acceptance out of his lips, she continued.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“I know it’s a lot to ask. I know your home is here, and Jo is here. I know we have a plan to protect her and that Felicity and Ezra are both extremely capable, but I… There’s no Knight I trust as much as I trust you, Hendrix. There’s no one better suited, other than Jo and myself, to watch over our daughter.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Of cour-”</em></p><p><em>“And I’ll pay you, of course. And provide an apartment. Anything you might-”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“My Queen.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>His voice was much more firm than he had ever given her. In normal circumstances, Hendrix wouldn’t have dreamed of talking to the Queen in such a tone, but he needed her to stop talking and let him answer. She quieted and met his eyes. In return, he offered her a small smile. He reached to the box of tissues on the coffee table that separated them and pulled several out to give her.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“You don’t need to explain or bargain with me, Your Majesty, I would go with Amelia for nothing.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Jo is-”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Jo would want me to protect you and your daughters. No matter what that means.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“But…”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Did you ask for this meeting without her because you were sure I wouldn’t leave her, or that she wouldn’t want me to leave?” The sheepish look on the Queen’s face was all the confirmation he needed. “Jo will understand. In fact, I believe she’d be more cross with me if I said no.”</em></p><p><em>“I think you might be right about that.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Of course I’ll go. Anything for you, my Queen.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>To his surprise, she leaned forward and took both of his hands in her own. He didn’t resist the touch. He squeezed her hands slightly as their eyes met. She was silent as she studied him. He wasn’t sure what she was looking for, but he would give her all the time she needed.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“I used to look at you and see only your mother, Hendrix. It’s hard not to, with that bright red hair of yours.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“I get that a lot,” Hendrix teased. When she smiled, just slightly, he grinned at her in return.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“But you’re him, too. And not just the eyes.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>Eilian gripped his hands and leaned forward to press a gentle kiss to his forehead. He closed his eyes for just a moment to take in the gentle touch. Now that he was grown, the affection was given less from those around him, but always appreciated nonetheless. Jo had long since stopped showing her affection for him in childish cuddles. He never doubted her love. She just had a different way of showing it.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Your father would be extremely proud of the man you’ve grown up to be, Hendrix.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Thank you, my Queen.”&nbsp;</em></p><p>Hendrix blinked and pulled himself out of his memories and back to the present. Ezra was still beside him. With one glance to the man, he could tell that they <em>both </em>knew that he hadn’t taken in a single word Ezra had said to him. Not, of course, that he cared what the man had to say.&nbsp;</p><p>“I’m <em>not</em> leaving,” Hendrix said firmly in order to put an end to Ezra’s continued insistence of his departure.&nbsp;</p><p>Though the order wasn’t specifically to be here <em>tonight</em>, their Queen had been clear: keep her safe and watch her grow up in all the ways Eilian couldn’t. He knew how much the Fae Queen wished she could be here to see her perform. Eilian wouldn’t have missed a recital or practice. She would have attended every parent teacher conference and made cookies for any bake sale. She did it for her other two children, but the prophesy meant that she wasn’t allowed the chance with Amelia. He knew it tore her apart.&nbsp;</p><p>She couldn’t be here, so he was. Even if Amelia never saw him. Even if Amelia didn’t know who he was. He was here.&nbsp;</p><p>He always would be.&nbsp;</p><p>“Your father would be ashamed of the Knight you’ve become,” Ezra shot at him. “He would-”&nbsp;</p><p>“No.”&nbsp;</p><p>Hendrix cut him off. He didn’t raise his voice or glare at him. He did what infuriated Ezra most: he remained behind the practiced neutral expressions he had learned from his aunt and straightened his posture like his father would have. He didn’t give him the benefit of a reaction because he could hear the Fae Queen’s words in his mind just as clearly now as he had that February day in her office.&nbsp;</p><p>“No. My father would be proud of me.”&nbsp;</p>
