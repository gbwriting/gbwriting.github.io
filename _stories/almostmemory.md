<blockquote data-alt="true" class="quote-alt">December 14th, 2011<p><strong>Reagan</strong></p></blockquote><p>“I’m <em>not </em>crazy.” </p><p>Her words were sharp as she leaned her forehead against the window. Reagan didn’t want to be here, though she knew the reasons that had made her end up here. The thick bandages on her arms had finally been removed, though they were still wrapped in gauze to keep Reagan from picking at the scabs. </p><p>“No one said you were, Reagan,” He replied. </p><p>His name was one that Reagan hadn’t committed to memory. Dr. Something, she knew that much. He was the head of her care in the psychiatric facility. She didn’t know if he was a psychiatrist, a therapist, or some combination of <em>both</em>. She didn’t care. She’d never asked. She probably wouldn’t.</p><p>Though she was an adult, Reagan knew the decisions weren’t fully her own anymore. At least, they weren’t while she was in the psychiatric center. It wasn’t a hospital. It was something they insisted on frequently throughout her care. To Reagan, it was all the same. Reagan couldn’t leave until they deemed her stable, and her mother was the one making all of the decisions for her. She didn’t resent it, though she <em>did </em>want to go home. </p><p>“You don’t have to say it,” She protested. “I know what you’re all thinking.” </p><p>“I’m thinking that it’s complicated,” He replied in the patient tone he always used with her. “I want to talk about what triggered your anxiety attack last night.” </p><p>“I don’t want to talk about it.” </p><p>“I do. I think we need to.” </p><p>“But-” </p><p>“Please, Reagan.” </p><p>Reagan closed her eyes. She remembered the stampede of elephants that paraded over her chest as she fought to breathe the night before. She remembered the sobs that shook her entire body as she clutched at her chest. It had felt like something was <em>missing</em>. It felt like something critical to who she <em>was</em>, but the issue was she couldn’t remember <em>what </em>it was. </p><p>“I felt like something was missing,” Reagan replied quietly. “Like someone had cut a piece out of my very soul.” </p><p>“Is that something you feel now?” </p><p>“Yes,” She murmured. Her fingers were running along the top of the gauze. “It’s something I <em>always </em>feel.” </p><p>“How long have you felt like this?” </p><p>“I don’t know.” </p><p>“When was the first time you remember feeling it?” </p><p>“I was nine,” Reagan whispered. She closed her eyes and pressed her forehead tighter against the glass pane of the window. The cold air outside made the glass cool against her forehead. It steadied her. “I remember having my first panic attack just after my Valentine’s Day party at school.” </p><p>“Do you remember what caused it?” He asked. </p><p>“No, not exactly. I remember running out of my class and looking for someone. I remember that I had a Valentine card in my hand for them. And then… I think I lost the card. I don’t remember, exactly. It’s a fuzzy memory.” </p><p>“And it feels like something is missing?” </p><p>“No,” Reagan whispered. “Not something.” </p><p>“What do you mean?” </p><p>“<em>Someone </em>is missing.” </p><p>“Was there a missing perso-” </p><p>“No, you don’t understand,” Reagan insisted. “There’s no actual person. Nobody that vanished. No missing child. I’ve asked my Mom a thousand times. It’s like I had an imaginary friend, and my mind stopped believing in them that day, but their absence is a physical ache that can’t be forgotten.” </p><p>He reached up to rub the bridge of his nose. Though he didn’t say it, she knew he was thinking she sounded insane. It was something she could understand, because she often felt it. It was why she didn’t speak of the shadowy figure she saw in some of her memories, or possibly her dreams. They were so hazy that she could never grasp. </p><p>They were always a variation of the same, though the location was rarely the same time. Sometimes, she was racing through the halls of Hawthorne Manor. Sometimes, it was a hedge maze of a garden. Other times, an empty playground. A beach. The park. The location varied, but the essence of what she saw was always the same. </p><p>In it, she was chasing after someone. She was a girl. From her shape, she was younger than Reagan. Reagan could always make out the long brown hair. It was partially tied back with a purple ribbon while the rest of it cascaded down her back. They were playing, and the girl’s laugh was always something she could hear like the most pleasant of melodies. Every time, just when she caught up to her, the girl would begin to turn, and then Reagan would be jolted from the memory right before she saw her face. </p><p><em>Who are you? </em></p><p><em>Why can’t I remember? </em></p><p><em>Are you real? </em></p><p><em>Or did I imagine you? </em></p><p>The older she became, the more she believed it was a delusion. Now, with the words she knew to be associated with her, she was convinced she had. This had to be a result of her diagnosis. She didn’t know much about Bipolar Disorder, but maybe it was the reason for every fucked up thing about her. </p><p>“Can you tell me what else you remember?” He asked. </p><p>Reagan turned her head to look at him. His dark brown eyes were patient, and he tilted his head in a way that she was growing to understand about the doctor’s here. It was pity, or maybe it was sympathy. Either way, Reagan didn’t want to talk about this anymore. She pushed herself fully to her feet. Their session wasn’t over, but she didn’t want to be here anymore. </p><p>“I don’t remember anything. It’s a stupid, imaginary thing I made up to entertain myself with as a child. There <em>was </em>no girl. No missing person. She was made up, and I have spent years being insane enough to convince myself she really existed.” </p><p>Panic rose in her chest, and she felt the tight, elephant feeling within her. She gripped the back of the chair she was meant to be sitting in so tightly her knuckles turned white. She had to push away the thoughts. She had to forget her. It was the only way she wouldn’t be swallowed whole. </p><p>“Please, I can’t-” She managed. “I can’t do this.” </p><p>“Okay, Reagan. We’ll stop for today.” </p><p>He took her arm to lead her out of his office. She was still struggling to breathe as he led her to her room. It was fully turning to panic when she was finally offered a tiny cup with pills that would calm her down and put her to sleep. </p><p>Reagan felt his presence fade away to be replaced with the nurses as they watched her. Her breathing was growing steadier. She laid back on the bed and turned to her side to face the window. The elephants were no longer crushing her, but the thoughts of the girl didn’t fade. They never did, at least not completely. </p><p><em>Who are you? </em></p><p><em>Why can’t I remember? </em></p><p>Reagan closed her eyes as she pulled the scratchy blankets over her shoulders and up to her chin. She fought to steady her breathing as she slipped into her own memory. She was in the garden this time, and she could catch the flash of brown hair around every turn. No matter how fast she ran, she couldn’t get her any closer. She couldn’t catch her. Reagan was sure she never would. </p><p><em>Were you real? </em></p><p><em>If you were…</em></p><p>W<em>hy did you leave me? </em></p>
