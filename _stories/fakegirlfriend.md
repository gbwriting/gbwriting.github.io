<blockquote class="quote-alt"><p><strong>Dahlia</strong></p></blockquote>

<p>Dahlia’s present level of excitement had the blonde practically vibrating.</p><p>Or maybe her wiggling demeanor was directly related to the four cups of coffee she’d had throughout the day and the vast amounts of caffeine currently pulsing through her veins. Either way, she was unable to sit still or focus on the piles of books that were spread across the long, bar top table in the back of Brewed Awakening where she was meant to be studying. Where her friends <em>were </em>diligently studying, despite her constant rattling of the table each time her boot knocked with the leg when she would shift positions.</p><p>Though she had her textbook open in front of her, it was covered up by her songwriting notebook as she tried, and failed, to write the set list for her very first show as an actual, serious musician.</p><p>Her first show <em>ever, </em>really, outside of the grandiose performances she would give as a preteen to her sister and their stuffed animals.</p><p>She’d been like this for weeks, ever since she’d received a call from a booking agent at The Nexus asking if she’d be interested in opening for a band from the Seattle Area whose <em>actual </em>opening act had a conflict for their Greenborough show. Dahlia, who adored music and followed nearly every band in the area, had never even heard of them, but apparently they’d heard of her.</p><p><em>“They saw your video,” Trace had said on the phone, the booking agent sounding tired. After years of going to </em><em>shows</em><em> at The Nexus, Dahlia knew that was Trace’s permanent state of being. Once, he’d told her it was the cost of selling his soul to rock and roll. “The one you put on YouTube.” </em></p><p><em>“They did?”</em></p><p><em>“Yeah. I watched it too. You’re good, kid. But anyway, they’re looking for someone. Plus side is that it’s a bit of exposure.” </em></p><p><em>“And the downside?”</em></p><p><em>“It pays nothing, but you get what you get,” Trace yawned, having delivered this news with the same energy one might deliver news of a traffic ticket, rather than something that could change Dahlia’s life. “I need to know by </em><em>end</em><em> of the—” </em></p><p><em>“I’ll take it.” </em></p><p>In that moment, it hadn’t mattered to Dahlia that she didn’t, technically, have a backing band or that she was wildly unprepared for an actual show. All that had mattered was that she had a real chance to prove herself as a musician. So she’d gathered a few willing classmates from her music program and booked out a practice room for any spare time the three of them had. </p><p>Maybe they weren’t <em>good</em>, but they didn’t totally <em>suck</em>.</p><p>At least, that was what Dahlia was repetitively telling herself as she tried to chase away the nausea that had settled in her stomach. Despite the earlier confidence, she was absolutely <em>terrified</em>. </p><p>“You’re going to be great, Dahl.” </p><p>Juniper’s voice pulled Dahlia out of her thoughts, pairing it with a featherlight touch to her forearm. Her head snapped up, finding her sister’s familiar icy gray eyes. She could count the thousands of ways that, despite being twins, they were different, but their eyes were never on that list. Juniper’s eyes matched her own, down to every last fleck of blue and long eyelash. </p><p>Those familiar eyes, paired with a soft smile, were the only thing keeping Dahlia’s rising anxiety from completely engulfing her. </p><p>“I <em>know</em>,” Dahlia commented. The words came out too quickly, her tone giving away that she felt anything but okay. “I’m <em>fine</em>. I’m just… set list. You know… gotta <em>have</em> one.” </p><p>“That’s it,” Ollie said from across the table, closing their own textbook with a thud and shoving it aside. “Dahlia, stop being stubborn and hand over your notebook.” </p><p>“But you have—” </p><p>“My homework can wait. <em>All </em>of our homework can wait. Hand it over, Blondie.” </p><p>Any further protests Dahlia had died on her lips as Ollie snatched her notebook from it’s resting place on her textbook. Beside them, Sutton closed her own notebook and leaned forward to get a better look. As was typical of the group, Ollie’s decision won out. When Ollie Knapp made a decision, it was often a mute point to protest against the stubborn brunette. </p><p>Not that Dahlia was in any position to argue their help right now, anyway. Whether she felt prepared or not, the show was a matter of hours away. She’d promised her backing band a set list by soundcheck. Checking her phone, Dahlia counted the two hours and fifteen minutes she had remaining as if a bomb would soon be going off. Even with the looming deadline, she was no closer to a decision. </p><p>“Walk us through it,” Sutton encouraged, putting a hand on Ollie’s leg as she leaned closer to her girlfriend. “What are you thinking?” </p><p>“Well. Um,” Dahlia replied. Her tongue felt as coarse and dry as sandpaper. “We’ve… rehearsed a lot of options.” </p><p>“Obviously, Kent,” Ollie teased with a wave of their hand. “What’s your opening song?” </p><p>“Well, um. I get five songs. So I… Probably a cover.” </p><p>“No way,” Juniper commented with a shake of her head. “You start with one of yours so you can show them how great you are.” </p><p>“Yeah, but… I could lose the crowd.”</p><p>“You’re just as likely to lose the crowd with a poor choice of a cover, too. They’ll think you’re just a cover band, and you are <em>not </em>a cover band, Dahlia Kent,” Ollie commented, reaching over to tap their index finger against Dahlia’s nose. “So not a cover. Or-rig-in-nal.” </p><p>Dahlia wrinkled her nose as Ollie quite literally tapped out their point with each syllable, swatting away her friend’s hand. They had a point, even if Dahlia wasn’t quite ready to give them that satisfaction. </p><p>“Okay. But <em>which </em>one?” </p><p>“You obviously have to close with ‘No Romeo’,” Sutton mused, earning an enthusiastic nod from both Ollie and Juniper. </p><p>“Not a cover,” Ollie echoed. </p><p>“I <em>get </em>it, Knapp.” </p><p>“Do you?” They chuckled. “I can keep reminding you.” </p><p>Of that, Dahlia was sure. </p><p>“Okay, so open with ‘Good Enough’, maybe?” Sutton continued, ignoring her girlfriend’s antics in favor of productivity, as was her typical approach to any situation in which Ollie felt particularly devilish. </p><p>“Oh, I love that one,” Juniper agreed, pulling the notebook towards her as she grabbed a pencil and started to erase Dahlia’s messy scrawl to replace it with her own neat lettering. “What about her cover of ‘Teenage Dirtbag’ to follow that up?”</p><p>“I <em>love </em>that, add it,” Sutton agreed. </p><p>“Do you three even need me for these decisions anymore?” Dahlia asked. </p><p>“No.” </p><p>“Not really.” </p><p>“Nah.” </p><p>The three of them, completely in sync, pulled a laugh from her as she shook her head. When the three of them were in agreement, she knew she stood no chance. She slid from her seat, grabbing her wallet from her bag. </p><p>“I’m going to get another coffee while you three plan my life for me.” </p><p>Dahlia barely waited for Juniper’s thumbs up before she left her friends to their own devices. Despite her teasing, she knew her set list was likely safer in their hands than in her own. At least <em>they </em>didn’t have the nagging self doubt clouding their decisions. They could be trusted with the important decisions, such as what songs would go on the setlist and which of her outfits would be suitable for going on stage. </p><p>Dahlia, however, would never be able to convince herself that any of her choices were good enough.</p><p>At first, performing at The Nexus had felt like something she could handle and do <em>well</em>. Now, it felt like a catastrophe waiting to happen. Her self doubt was looping in her mind, only growing louder and louder as it began to settle into a full blown panic in her stomach. No matter how much she tried to hide it, Dahlia knew that her friends would see it, though she let herself hide in her doubt as she placed her coffee order. </p><p>She was in a daze throughout the transaction, barely coming back to earth in time to hand over her card to the cashier, who was staring just as absently towards the other end of the counter. After several seconds of awkwardly extending her arm, he focused took it from her. </p><p>“We’ll have that in just a second,” He commented as he set her receipt and card back on the counter instead of her outstretched hand. Dahlia barely had time to reach for it before he abandoned her in favor of laughing under his breath to the other barista, who was focused on making drinks. </p><p>“Uh, okay.” </p><p>Dahlia shook her head, tucking her card back into the front pocket of her jeans before she walked to the other side of the counter. Her phone immediately came back out as she studied the group chat from the classmates who made up her so-called band this evening. They weren’t nervous, which objectively *was* a good thing, though Dahlia was aware they had much less to lose than she did. </p><p>“Medium iced caramel macchiato with almond milk, up!” </p><p>Dahlia didn’t glance up from her phone as she stepped forward and scooped the cup off the counter. Juniper, who much preferred drinks so sweet they barely resembled coffee at all, had once commented that no one liked <em>her</em> drink, especially with the addition of non-dairy milk, and preferred the elaborate drink recipes the cafe came up with to match whatever holiday or event was coming up. </p><p>To Juniper’s credit, that <em>did </em>tend to be true. </p><p>“Hey! That’s <em>my </em>drink.” </p><p>At first, Dahlia didn’t consider that the annoyed tone could be directed at her. Still, the blonde turned with the drink hovering just below her lips to confirm. She expected to see another drink thieving offender, but instead, she was presented with a woman with red hair.</p><p>A <em>beautiful </em>woman with red hair who was glaring at her with hands poised on her hips in a way that confirmed <em>Dahlia </em>was the reason for her annoyance. </p><p>“Erm,” Dahlia managed, her breath hitching in her throat as she glanced away from her to the drink in her hand. The tag labeled it as an iced caramel macchiato with almond milk, just as she’d predicted. Her drink. Right? “I think, possibly, that you’re mistaken. Unless you’re trying to do a really cheesy pick up line.” </p><p>For a split second, the redhead’s sharp look softened, though the annoyed one that preceded it returned nearly immediately. She crossed her arms over her chest, eyes narrowing slightly in a way that was more mischievous than bothered. Then, to Dahlia’s delight, her lip twitched into a smirk that was significantly more playful. </p><p>“Please, if I was trying to pick you up, you would already be in my bed.” </p><p>“Uh.” </p><p><em>Uh</em> was hardly an answer, but this beautiful, confident woman seemed to take away every coherent line of thought from Dahlia. Worse, she seemed to be enjoying it. Worst of all, <em>Dahlia</em> liked it. </p><p>“Is… is that so?” Dahlia replied, trying her best to sound smooth and confident. She stood up a little straighter as their eyes met. “And um—” </p><p>“Caramel Macchiato with almond milk for Dahlia!”</p><p>Dahlia had no idea what would have come out of her mouth, but whatever response she’d been working up to was interrupted by the barista as another drink was placed on the counter. A drink that was identical to the one in her hand. Which meant…</p><p><em>Shite. </em>She <em>had </em>stolen her drink. </p><p>Which could have all been avoided if, the <em>first </em>time, the barista had called out a name. Dahlia was confident she hadn’t, and she frowned as she glanced down at the black scrawl on the side of the cup in her hand. In an instant, the distracted nature of the cashier started to make <em>much </em>more sense. </p><p>How could she salvage this? </p><p>“Well, it looks like I got yours by mistake,” Dahlia mused, motioning to the one that was rightfully her own on the counter, then to the one in her hand. “Since she didn’t call out your name, I just scooped it up and assumed it was my own, since this is what I typically order and usually people enjoy the gross shite.” </p><p>“I <em>told </em>you so.” </p><p>That had gotten a little bit of a smile, though the redhead didn’t drop her arms away from their crossed position across her chest. Dahlia had no idea of <em>why </em>the cup said what it did, but she was determined to deliver the news in a way that didn’t make her seem like a dick. It felt impossible, and Dahlia had a feeling this would be her biggest performance of the night. </p><p>“Though um, I can see the confusion, because it’s usually pretty socially acceptable to say my name, <em>Dahlia</em>, in public, but your name… which… forgive my pronunciation…” Dahlia tilted her head dramatically as she studied the cup. “Lying… Fucking Bitch… might be a little harder to call in public, no? What’s <em>that </em>about?” </p><p>Just as Dahlia predicted, there was no way to tell her what was written on the cup without hurting her, and that was the last thing she’d wanted to do. Her stomach sank as the redhead’s jaw tightened, turning back to the counter in time to see the two baristas burst into cruel laughter. Dahlia watched in horror as the petite brunette who had been making drinks locked eyes with the redhead. </p><p>“That’s for leading me on.” </p><p>Though the words were mouthed instead of being spoken out loud, it was impossible to misread what she’d said. Dahlia’s own jaw tightened as she watched the way the confident woman in front of her deflated. Arms uncrossed as she rubbed her forehead, sighing softly before she took a deep breath and steadied herself. When she finally looked back to Dahlia, her smile was a tense one, not the playful one Dahlia already found herself preferring. </p><p>“It’s… stupid. She asked me out to drinks and I thought it was as friends, but she had other ideas,” She explained. Before Dahlia could reassure her that she didn’t owe her any more of an explanation, she continued. “For the record, I didn’t lead her on or give her any false hope. I mean, you’ve known me for two seconds and I’ve made it very clear that I was flirting with you.” </p><p>So she <em>was </em>flirting with her. </p><p>“I didn’t do that with her,” She continued. “But I guess she thought I was.” </p><p>“Even if you did lead her on, that’s uncalled for.”</p><p>“Yeah… well, you know how it goes,” She sighed. “I definitely don’t want this now.” </p><p>Dahlia could only stare at her as the drink was pulled from her hand and deposited into a nearby trash can. With the defeated way her shoulders slumped, it was clear that she had no intention of ordering another drink. When she picked up Dahlia’s drink and held it out to her, all the blonde could do was stare at her. </p><p>“I don’t want to risk drinking poison.” </p><p>In that moment, Dahlia knew she couldn’t let this woman’s day go like this. </p><p>Not when she was <em>easily</em> the most beautiful woman Dahlia had ever seen. </p><p>Truthfully, Dahlia was a damn sucker for girls exactly like this mysterious redhead. With her long, immaculately styled coppery red hair and hazel eyes that sparked not just with mischief, but with flecks of pure gold, Dahlia didn’t stand a fucking chance. Her eyes scanned the other girl, taking in every inch of her, from her perfectly manicured nails down to her edgy, stylish clothing that dared anyone to fuck with her.</p><p>And Dahlia…</p><p><em>Fuck</em>, Dahlia was aware that even if the moment ended right here, she would spend the next two weeks concocting elaborate scenarios about in her own mind. She would dwell on the sort of scenarios that might put her and this gorgeous girl in a path to the sort of romance she’d <em>never </em>admit she craved out loud. Her friends would never let her live it down if she did. She could see Ollie’s playful laugh, Juniper’s smug smile, and Sutton’s silent, yet piercing, look now. </p><p>This girl…</p><p>She was the sort of woman that could make Dahlia completely lose her shit. </p><p>Which was why, with no real plan for what would come next, Dahlia stepped up to the counter and smacked her palm against the smooth top to get their attention. </p><p>“<em>Oi!</em> Where do you get off being a bloody cunt to my girlfriend?” </p><p>“Excuse me?” The brunette, whose name tag labeled her as Kiara, snapped as she turned to face Dahlia. Even with a counter between them, their difference in height meant she craned her neck to look up at the blonde. </p><p>“Remake her drink. The <em>right </em>way.” </p><p>“I <em>did </em>make it the right way.” </p><p>“Then you’re even more daft than you look,” Dahlia replied without missing a beat. “Fix it. Now.”</p><p>“And what if I don’t?”</p><p>“Dahl-” </p><p>The redhead had stepped up to the counter with her, tugging on Dahlia’s arm in an attempt to pull her away. As if to signal that this wasn’t worth the effort of protecting her. Maybe it was a little thing, really, but to Dahlia, the defense was worth it. Even if she had no idea how to be intimidating in the way this situation clearly warranted. </p><p>“Well. Erm,” She frowned. “If you <em>don’t…” </em></p><p>Dahlia’s eyes scanned her immediate surroundings, trying to come up with some sort of retort to that. She hadn’t anticipated the push back, and this was far more in the realm of what Ollie would handle flawlessly. Certainly not <em>her</em>. Despite her height, Dahlia was aware that she wasn’t exactly intimidating. </p><p>When her blue eyes fell on a ceramic pumpkin the staff was currently using a tip jar, she smirked and placed her hand on top of it. She moved it a half inch closer to the edge of the counter as she looked back to Kiara. </p><p>“If you don’t, then the pumpkin gets it.” </p><p><em>The pumpkin gets it?</em></p><p>The instant the words came from her lips, Dahlia knew this would end up being one of those sentences that her friends would laughingly – and lovingly – quote until the end of time. She could already envision the way that Ollie would mime the slaughter of the tip jar, or her complete lack of game. Dahlia would probably laugh and agree that this was <em>precisely </em>the reason why she didn’t have a girlfriend. All because her idea of being threatening and standing up for her girlfriend was to… threaten the life of a tip jar. </p><p>So very, <em>very</em><em> </em>rock and roll. </p><p>“She’s not worth it, babe.” </p><p>The words from the redhead, accompanied by a firm squeeze to her hand, caught her attention. Dahlia turned just in time for her to put her hand on her cheek. At first, she thought it was just a way to get her attention, but the way soft fingertips stroked over her skin made her heart skip. Her skin felt like it was on fire with a single touch. Instinctively, she let out a shaky breath as she leaned in. This was part of the game, she knew that logically, but she would be a damn liar if she said she didn’t absolutely love it. </p><p>“Yes, but—” </p><p>Dahlia’s words were cut off by the redhead suddenly pressing herself up on her toes, closing the distance between them. The touch of her lips with Dahlia’s own made her feel like she had been bathed in sunlight. The world felt just a little bit brighter in those few seconds. Brighter and blinding, centering around the woman in front of her. She didn’t let go of her hand, but her free hand came to rest on her waist, drawing her in and hoping it felt natural. </p><p>The kiss wasn’t more than a tender, rather chaste peck. Even still, when they broke, the little noise that escaped Dahlia’s lips was involuntary. That tiny whine was barely audible to anyone that wasn’t the two of them, but the glint in her eyes told Dahlia she’d been caught. </p><p>She didn’t even care. </p><p>“Y-you’re right,” She managed, hoping her voice sounded more steady than she felt and her cheeks weren’t too red. “N-not worth it.” </p><p>It felt impossible to pull her eyes away from the redhead, but she somehow managed to glance at the barista, who looked even more shocked than Dahlia felt. She might have been imagining it, but Dahlia <em>swore </em>Kiara was gripping the can of whipped cream a little too tightly. </p><p>“Not even a little bit,” the redhead agreed with the sort of smile that made Dahlia weak in the knees. </p><p>“You can have mine,” Dahlia insisted. She scooped her coffee up off the counter and pressed it into her hands. “We’ll just have to share.” </p><p>“I <em>definitely</em> don’t mind sharing with you.” </p><p>“Good. Come on, babe,” Dahlia took her hand, lacing their fingers in a way that felt too natural for strangers. Maybe it would pass as them actually being in a relationship long enough for them to get away from Kiara’s glare. She led her away from the counter, though not without taking one last shot at the barista. “You know, you were right. She really <em>does </em>look like that naked mole rat from <em>Kim Possible</em>.” </p><p>Dahlia’s comment might have been a bit immature, but the snort of laughter that left the redhead’s lips was electrifying. She smiled even brighter as they walked away. Dahlia didn’t bother looking back at Kiara. Doing so would only risk blowing the carefully crafted illusion they’d managed, in which she got to walk away from this situation while the bitter barista only looked like a fool. Dahlia got to play the part of the doting girlfriend who would avenge her honor by slaying cruel women and, apparently, ceramic pumpkins. </p><p>All before she even knew the beautiful girl’s name. </p><p>“Thank you for doing that. You didn’t have to, but I really appreciate it,” the redhead commented when they were out of earshot of the baristas. </p><p>“No, I totally had to do it. Besides, I can’t just let someone trash <em>my</em> girl like that.” </p><p>Dahlia hadn’t been focused on the destination of where she was leading her to, though as the words left her lips she realized she had led her directly back to the table of her three open mouthed friends, who had clearly witnessed the entire thing. She hadn’t thought about the implications of leading her over to the table. It had felt so natural to walk in that direction, but now that she was standing in front of her friends, she felt the heat in her cheeks. She knew better than to look at Ollie’s inquisitive stare, and she’d certainly lose her shit if she met Juniper’s gaze, so she settled on Sutton’s face as she lifted their entwined hands slightly. </p><p>“Hey guys. Have you met my girlfriend? This is…” She let out a sheepish laugh as she glanced to the girl at her side. “You know, I can’t really call you by the drink name forever. Should I go by your initials? FLB?” </p><p>“Yardley. I’m Yardley Parker.” </p><p><em>Yardley.</em> Was there a more perfect name in existence? Dahlia knew that name would be one she’d never forget. She mouthed the name back to her, though no sound left her lips. She was still holding her hand, and perhaps it was time to let go, but neither of them did. Dahlia didn’t want to. Desperately, she hoped that Yardley didn’t either. </p><p>“I um,” Yardley managed with a smile that seemed much more shy than she’d shown before. “Thanks again. I should… I should let you hang out with your friends.” </p><p>She didn’t let go. </p><p>Dahlia didn’t either. </p><p>“They could be your friends too,” Dahlia blurted out. She could practically hear the smirk that she knew was on Ollie’s face, but she didn’t look away from Yardley. “If… If you wanted to sit with us. I mean… I’d… I’d really like…” </p><p>Dahlia couldn’t finish the sentence. Her stomach felt like it was filled with butterflies and her tongue was impossibly dry. She could only imagine how red her cheeks were. She couldn’t speak. Instead, she just gaped at the woman in front of her. </p><p>“We’d like it, too,” Juniper finished, speaking up for her tongue tied twin. She leaned across the table, extending her hand to Yardley. As they shook hands, Dahlia let out a breath she didn’t realize she was holding as her sister took charge. </p><p>“It’s nice to meet you, Yardley. I’m Juniper, and this is Sutton,” Juniper paused long enough for Sutton to wave before motioning to Ollie. “And Sutton’s girlfriend, Ollie.” </p><p>“It’s nice to meet all of you.” </p><p>It seemed to happen in slow motion. Dahlia watched as Ollie moved their backpack off of the chair beside her own empty one. Sutton moved the stack of textbooks from the space on the table. Juniper motioned to the empty spaces. Dahlia and Yardley both sat down, hands still entwined. </p><p>“You don’t mind?” Yardley asked. “I don’t want to interrupt.” </p><p>“You’re not interrupting,” Juniper replied with a wave of her hand before she slid the notebook – Dahlia’s notebook – to the middle of the table. The show. Dahlia had completely forgotten about it. “We were just working out the set list for Dahl’s show later. If you’d like direct input, I can definitely score an extra ticket for later.” </p><p>Was her <em>sister </em>asking Yardley on a date for her? One glance at Sutton’s smirk told Dahlia that yes, yes she was. Could her own lack of game be any more evident? Should she have asked her already to the show? Should she say something now? </p><p>“You didn’t tell me you had a show, babe,” Yardley teased, giving her hand another squeeze as their eyes met. The playful smile was mixed with a genuine one that made her heart skip a beat. “I obviously need to hear about this show and setlist now.” </p><p>Dahlia felt her cheeks burning at Yardley’s words. The endearment, which was starting to feel a little less like a show, though that could have been Dahlia’s hopes more than anything real, warmed her from the inside out. She liked it far more than she would have thought after knowing her for such a short period of time. She <em>really </em>liked the way Yardley smiled at her. She especially liked the way she teased her. </p><p>“I um. It’s…” Dahlia’s eyes flitted to Juniper, who waved her on with a flick of her wrist. She couldn’t figure out how to get her voice to work. “It’s… It’s just a show.” </p><p>“It’s <em>more </em>than <em>just </em>a show,” Ollie chimed in with an encouraging smile. “Dahl, since when are you so <em>shy</em>?” </p><p>Juniper’s sharp look silenced them before eyes, an identical blue to Dahlia’s own, found Yardley. “Ollie is right, it’s more than just a show. Dahlia’s a musician and is playing her first real show tonight.” </p><p>“I’m an <em>aspiring </em>musician,” Dahlia corrected, cheeks going darker. If possible, she would have sank into the ground to hide from how nervous this conversation made her. Would Yardley want to come? If she did, would she be impressed with Dahlia’s music? “It’s… um. It’s just an opening. And it’s only like one or two original songs. Mostly covers. It’s… not a big deal.” </p><p>“It’s a big deal,” Sutton corrected, leaning across the table to squeeze Dahlia’s arm. “It’s huge, and we’re so proud of her.” </p><p>“I think I have to agree with your friends,” Yardley mused, eyes meeting Dahlia’s own once more. Playfulness glinted in her hazel eyes, though something else was present, too. Either way, it was enough to make her stomach flip. “I think it sounds amazing.” </p><p>“Yeah? I… I mean, it’s exciting,” Dahlia managed.
