<blockquote class="quote-alt"><p>September 4th, 2006</p><p><strong>Charlie</strong></p></blockquote><p>“Fuck! Holy… Fuck!”&nbsp;</p><p>Three things caught Charlotte Mackenzie’s attention at the same time: the loud crunching noise, the sound of her mother’s curse, and the tight way her seatbelt felt across her chest as the car quickly came to a stop. The sudden motion vaulted the book she had been reading from her lap and onto the floor. From her booster seat, Charlotte didn’t know the reason for the abrupt stop, though the string of curses that left her mother’s lips made her sit up straighter.&nbsp;</p><p>“Mommy?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Shit. Shit.”&nbsp;</p><p>Just outside the side window, Charlotte could see the car that had been in front of them. Just minutes ago, she was talking about how much she loved the color of it: blue, but not <em>too </em>blue. Now, it was upside down and against a tree. The front of it was smashed. The sight of it made Charlotte clutch at the seatbelt, desperate for her mother’s attention.&nbsp;</p><p>“<em>Mommy!”&nbsp;</em></p><p>Her voice was sharp and panicked. The sound of it pulled her mother’s attention from the car to the rearview mirror. Eyes that matched her own met Charlotte’s, and she could see how scared her mother was. It only made her more afraid.&nbsp;</p><p>“Lottie, I need-” Michelle’s voice was quiet as she looked at her daughter, then back to the side of the road. All the while, Charlotte didn’t move.&nbsp;</p><p>“That car-”&nbsp;</p><p>“I know. Listen to me.”&nbsp;</p><p>Michelle unbuckled her seatbelt, which she had once said Charlotte was <em>never </em>to do while the car was on, and climbed into the backseat. She reached to grab ahold of her daughter’s hands and placed them over her eyes.&nbsp;</p><p>“Lottie, I need you to stay in the car and keep your eyes closed until I tell you to open them, okay?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Why-”&nbsp;</p><p>“Lottie, I need to help those people. And in order to help them, I need to know you’re safe. Can you do that?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Yes-”&nbsp;</p><p>“Lottie, who do we call when there’s an emergency?”&nbsp;</p><p>Charlotte nearly moved her hands, but thought better of it. They were pressed tighter against her face. She didn’t understand, but her mother told her to keep her eyes closed, and she always did what she was told. Instead, she bit on her lower lip.&nbsp;</p><p>“We call 911,” She whispered.&nbsp;</p><p>“Good girl. I’m going to dial 911, and you’re going to talk to them, okay? You’re going to tell them that there was a car accident on Highway 5, and you’re going to stay on the phone with them until they hang up, okay? And what are you going to do?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Keep my eyes closed.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Good girl,” Michelle murmured as she pressed a kiss to her daughter’s head.&nbsp;</p><p>From the backseat, Charlotte heard her press buttons on her phone. It echoed in the car over the speakers. She heard the sound of the door opening and the whistle of the wind. The door shut. Charlotte kept her hands over her eyes.&nbsp;</p><p>“911, what’s your emergency?”&nbsp;</p><p>The woman’s voice nearly made her jump out of her skin. Already, Charlotte wanted her mother to come back. She wanted her to tell her what to do. Talking to the stranger made her chest feel funny.&nbsp;</p><p>“Th.. There’s b… been an accident.”&nbsp;</p><p>Charlotte’s voice sounded tiny. <em>Was that what Mommy wanted me to say?</em> She couldn’t remember. She wanted her mom. She wanted her to come back.&nbsp;</p><p>“What’s your name, honey?” The woman’s voice was kind, but it didn’t bring any comfort to her. Only her mother would do that, but she was no longer in the car.&nbsp;</p><p>“Ch… Charlotte Rose Mackenzie.”</p><p>“Hi Charlotte, I’m Rebecca. Are you hurt?”&nbsp;</p><p>“No. Not me.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Where was the accident?”&nbsp;</p><p>“On… on…”&nbsp;</p><p>“It’s okay. Do you remember?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Highway… five?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Okay, good.&nbsp; Are your parents with you?”&nbsp;</p><p>“My… My mommy is helping save people. She… She’s a doctor.”</p><p>“Your Mommy is <em>really </em>brave-”&nbsp;</p><p>“I know.”&nbsp;</p><p>There was a louder noise outside the car and the sound of a scream. Charlotte whimpered and pressed her hands tighter over her eyes. She didn’t know what was happening, and while she wanted to look, she had been told not to. Her mother told her to keep her hands over her eyes, and that was what she was going to do.&nbsp;</p><p>Charlotte Mackenzie <em>always </em>did what she was told.&nbsp;</p><p>“Charlotte, are you alright?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Yes.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Do you know what that noise was?”&nbsp;</p><p>“No.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Where are you right now?”&nbsp;</p><p>“In my booster seat.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Do you think you can peek out the window and tell me what you see?”&nbsp;</p><p>Charlotte wanted to do what Rebecca asked of her, but her mother’s instructions won out. She squeezed her eyes shut under her hands, just to be extra sure. She wanted to know what the noises were outside and where her mother was, but she couldn’t disobey.&nbsp;</p><p>“No. My Mommy told me to keep my hands over my eyes and not to look, no matter what.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Your Mommy is very smart. Can you tell me what you can hear, Charlotte?”</p><p>“Yeah,” Charlie whispered. She sat up a little straighter to try to focus on her task. As a Were, she had <em>very </em>good hearing. “I hear… this… crackling noise. And I hear people talking. And… sirens. Police, I think? No. Fire trucks. My Daddy taught me the difference.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Good job, that means the police are <em>right </em>behind them.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Okay. Good.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Do you hear anything else?”&nbsp;</p><p>“No, just you. No, wait. I hear…” Charlotte squeezed her eyes shut tighter. The noises outside the car were growing louder. More voices poured out, and she was trying to pick out individual words.&nbsp;</p><p><em>“On fire - did they get anyone out?”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Girl, approximately five years old. They’re doing CPR.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Anyone else?”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“No.”&nbsp;</em></p><p>“The… the car is on… fire?” Charlotte’s voice was timid as she spoke, the question easily heard in her voice. “Not… <em>my </em>car. I think my Mom is doing CPR.”&nbsp;</p><p>The sound of the wind intensified once more. Charlotte felt the cool air on her face and realized the car door was open. Hope bloomed within the child. She started to move her hands when she heard the male voice speak out to her. It was within the car, and she pressed her hands tighter.&nbsp;</p><p>“Hi Charlotte. My name is Officer Lewis. I’m a police officer.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Hi…” She whispered.&nbsp;</p><p>“You can take your hands off your eyes if you want to.”&nbsp;</p><p>“No,” Charlotte shook her head and squeezed her eyes even tighter shut. “My mom said to keep them shut until she told me to open them.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Charlotte, are you alright?” It was Rebecca’s voice now, and she bit her lip. She liked Rebecca. She trusted her, even if they never met. Her calm voice made her feel safe.&nbsp;</p><p>“Yes, but I’m keeping my eyes closed.”&nbsp;</p><p>“That’s okay, honey,” Officer Lewis replied. “Your Mom is riding in the ambulance to the hospital. She’s a hero.”&nbsp;</p><p>“She’s a <em>doctor</em>,” She corrected.&nbsp;</p><p>“Some people say that’s the same thing,” He chuckled, but she didn’t see anything funny about this. “Your Mom asked me to drive you to the hospital, okay?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Okay.”&nbsp;</p><p>Charlotte heard the conversation between Rebecca and the Officer, though she didn’t understand the big words. Unresponsive. Cardiac Arrest. Fatal. She wanted to ask, but she was too scared to interrupt. Instead, she kicked her feet slightly as she tried to take in each word. All the while, Charlotte kept her hands firmly over her eyes.&nbsp;</p><p>“It was nice to talk to you, Charlotte. You were so brave,” Rebecca said.&nbsp;</p><p>“I didn’t do anything.”&nbsp;</p><p>Charlotte never understood when adults said things like that. She was sitting here. She sat in her booster seat <em>every</em> day. What had she done differently today than any other day? Was riding in the car <em>always </em>brave? Her words made them laugh. She didn’t understand why. She rarely understood why adults laughed.&nbsp;</p><p>Charlotte heard the phone hang up and the car door shut. Next came the click of the seatbelt and the sliding noise that meant he scooted the seat back. Her mother wouldn’t be happy with that. She was always annoyed when her father moved the seat, and especially when he moved the mirrors.&nbsp;</p><p>“My mom won’t like you moving the seat.”&nbsp;</p><p>“She said it was okay,” Officer Lewis chuckled. Again, he laughed at her. Again, she didn’t understand why. “You can take your hands off your eyes if you want to.”&nbsp;</p><p>“My Mom said not to.”</p><p>She felt the car start to go. It was slow at first, then steady as they picked up on the highway. He turned up the radio. She didn’t know the song, but he must have liked it. He hummed along to the music and tapped his hand against the steering wheel. Charlotte bounced her leg and chewed on her lower lip. They were two things her father didn’t like her to do, but she didn’t know what else to do.</p><p>It felt like an eternity before the car came to a stop. She knew he turned it off because the radio stopped and the car made the dinging sound that her mother said she needed to remember to call someone about but never did. She heard the click of a seatbelt coming undone before, finally, the officer spoke to her again.&nbsp;</p><p>“We can get out of the car now, we’ll wait inside.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Mom said to-”&nbsp;</p><p>“Your mom will be inside.”&nbsp;</p><p>“No.” Her voice was firm as she pressed herself back in her seat. With her eyes covered, she couldn’t see his face, but she knew he was frowning. He did the sighing thing her father always did when he frowned. “My Mom said to close my eyes and stay in the car.”&nbsp;</p><p>She heard him get out of the car. Outside, several voices spoke over each other. She couldn’t make out a single one of them. She wanted to peek. She wanted to know if the impatient officer left or if her mother was returning to her. She wanted to see, but her mother had said to keep her eyes covered, and Charlotte <em>always </em>did as she was told.</p><p>“Is she alright?”&nbsp;</p><p>The voice came what seemed like a <em>long </em>time later, and she sat up a little straighter. She knew that voice. Her mother. She sounded tired and worried, maybe even out of breath in the way she always was when she came back from a morning run, but it was <em>her</em>.&nbsp;</p><p>The car door opened, this time <em>her </em>door, and she smelled the sweet scent of her mother’s perfume. Then, the light touch of her fingertips on the girl’s arm. Slowly, her hands were pulled away from her eyes and she blinked at the bright sunlight. Her mother <em>was </em>tired and she had a little bit of blood on her shirt, but Charlotte immediately felt safer in her presence. They were at the hospital. She knew it well.</p><p>“I kept my eyes closed,” She whispered as she met the blue eyes that were the same shade as her own.&nbsp;</p><p>“I know,” Michelle smiled. She tucked Charlotte’s hair behind her ear and kissed her forehead. She reached to unbuckle her booster seat and this time, Charlotte didn’t protest. She took her mother’s hand and slid from the car.&nbsp;</p><p>“You did so good, Lottie. Let’s go inside.”</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p><br></p><p><br></p><p><br></p>
