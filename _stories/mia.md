<blockquote class="quote-alt"><p>September 16th, 2016</p><p><strong>Tessa</strong></p></blockquote><p>“What are you doing here?”&nbsp;</p><p>Tessa didn’t recognize the sound of her own voice. The noise that left her lips sounded frail and weak, a complete opposite to the confidence she prided herself in. If she had <em>any </em>tears left to shed, the croakiness of her voice might have been her undoing. As it was, Tessa didn’t think she could produce a single tear.&nbsp;</p><p>“I came to check on you.”&nbsp;</p><p>Tessa’s dark eyes lifted to look at the Warlock as he knelt in front of her on the impossibly white tile floor. Any other time, the greeting between Lachlan and herself would be laced with sarcasm and playful teasing. She might have had something clever to say about his pressed blazer, or he would say something sarcastic about the way her hair looked like she hadn’t brushed it in days. Under normal, everyday circumstances, Tessa wouldn’t have been greeted by the concerned look in his eyes.&nbsp;</p><p><em>These are not normal circumstances.&nbsp;</em></p><p>Tessa didn’t fight him as he reached up to smooth down flyaway pieces of her hair. He kept stroking her hair and she leaned into the touch. Even if she wanted to fight him on the comfort he was trying to provide her, she didn’t have the energy. Instead, she closed her eyes when she felt more tears beginning to form. Her fingernails bit crescent moons into her palms as she squeezed her hand tight. The pain felt far away. It didn’t help.</p><p>“It’s your birthday.”&nbsp;</p><p>“I know,” Lachlan murmured. “That doesn’t matter.”&nbsp;</p><p>“It matters.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Not right now, it doesn’t.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Okay.”&nbsp;</p><p>Tessa opened her eyes and peered into the blue-green eyes of her best friend. He looked impossibly worried, and she wished she could reassure him that everything was fine. As it was, the Witch wasn’t so sure that it would be. In the past thirty-two impossible hours, everything inside of her told her it <em>wouldn’t</em>. No matter how many times she was reassured to trust the process, Tessa couldn’t believe it would work out in her favor.&nbsp;</p><p><em>Why should I believe it will?</em></p><p><em>It never has before.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>But please… Please let it work out this time.&nbsp;</em></p><p>“How is she?” Lachlan murmured. With the tone in his voice, Tessa knew he already knew as much as she did: absolutely nothing.</p><p>“I don’t know. They won’t tell me anything.”&nbsp;</p><p>“I’m so sorry, Tess,” Lachlan whispered. “Not even Jackson?”&nbsp;</p><p>“No,” She sighed. Fingers came to run through her tangled brunette locks. “Especially not him. He’s taken himself completely off the case. He doesn’t want…”&nbsp;</p><p>“That’s probably a <em>good </em>thing, Tess. No conflict of interest.”</p><p>“I know.”</p><p>Tessa looked away from him. Instead, she looked across the small waiting room to the glass window that looked into the NICU. She had lost count of the number of hours she had spent pressed against the glass for a glimpse at the baby girl in the glass box hooked up to an impossible number of machines. She didn’t know what each machine did or what the cords ran to, but she did know that the biggest, scariest one was breathing <em>for </em>her.</p><p><em>Her</em>. The little, nameless baby who already held Tessa’s heart in her tiny fingers, even if Tessa had yet to be allowed to see, touch, or hold her. Tessa’s very <em>soul </em>ached for the little girl fighting for her life inside the NICU. In her eyes, she had been <em>hers </em>from the very moment she found out about her existence. The problem was that the court didn’t think so, at least not yet.&nbsp;</p><p><em>“Theresa! I have wonderful news for you!”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>Tessa’s groceries were placed on the counter as she turned to look at her sister, who had appeared when she arrived home and followed her inside. As always, she wasn’t invited into the home, though that never stopped her. With one look at the younger Witch, Tessa could tell she was absolutely gone.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“What are you on, Clara?” She asked.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Tess,” Clara waved off her concerns with a flick of her wrist. “That is not why I’m here.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“And yet, the question remains,” Tessa crossed her arms over her chest. In her head, she was already calculating her budget. How much would Clara ask for this time? How much could she afford to give? “What are you on?”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Coke. It’s nothing, really.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>Cocaine. That’s what it always was, at least in the start. A little coke would quickly turn into ecstasy. Ecstasy would turn into meth. When the thrill of that wore off, it would become the Hunter drugs that had drawn Clara to Greenborough after her sister in the first place. It would continue until she was in the hospital or arrested.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>It was the same every time.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>Every goddamn time, Tessa fell for the promises that she would change or do better. Every time, she knew better. That knowledge didn’t stop how much she ached when she looked into her sister’s eyes and saw the promising thirteen year old Honor Roll student she had once been, who dreamed of law school just like Tessa. Now, she was a high school dropout following in the footsteps of their parents, not her older sister.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>Still, Tessa wanted the only family she’d ever had back.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Do you want the good news or not, Theresa!”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Sure,” Tessa sighed, running her fingers through her hair. Zoe would be home any minute. Zoe would hate to see Clara again. She hated when Tessa gave in to the girl’s demands. It would be a fight again. “What’s the news?”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“I’m pregnant!”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“I…” Tessa couldn’t breathe. All she could do was stare at her sister completely dumbfounded. She grabbed her keys. “Okay. Come on.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Where are we going?”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“We need to get a pregnancy test for you, so we can confirm, and then we can talk about options. Adoption… abortio-”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“I’m keeping it, Tess.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>Tessa froze in place as she looked to her sister. She was impossibly thin, a direct result of the drugs she pumped into her system. Her clothing was torn and dirty. Her hair was impossibly oily. Her skin was grey and hollow, and her teeth were yellow and damaged. Even though she was three years younger than Tessa, she looked a decade older.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>Clara couldn’t take care of herself, let alone anyone else. She imagined a helpless baby in this situation and felt sick. She imagined herself at two years old, at four, at six, and finally at eight when they were removed from their parents’ home. She remembered it all in vivid, terrifying details.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>This will not happen again.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Like hell you are,” Tessa said firmly, shaking her head. “You can’t-”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“You can’t stop me,” Clara put her hand on her still flat stomach. “I’m going to be an amazing mother. You’ll see.”&nbsp;</em></p><p>Lachlan’s touch to her arm brought her back from her thoughts. She blinked several times to clear her mind as she refocused on the present and not the argument that started this whole ordeal or the dozens of conversations that followed that one. In every argument with her sister over the past six and a half months, Clara had insisted she would be an incredible mother all while being so high that she could barely form words.&nbsp;</p><p>“Tess, do you want me to go see what I can find out?” Lachlan asked. His voice was impossibly quiet.&nbsp;</p><p>“No, you should be at home with your wife. It’s your <em>birthday</em>.”&nbsp;</p><p>“I don’t give a fuck about my birthday. I care about <em>you</em>, Tess.”&nbsp;</p><p>“But it’s your-”&nbsp;</p><p>“Tess,” Lachlan put his hands on either side of her shoulders to hold on to her upper arms. She wanted to look away from him, but she couldn’t bring herself to. She hated that her friends were all seeing her crumble in on herself, but there wasn’t a damn thing she could do to stop it. “We’ll celebrate my birthday next year. Me, you, and the little one.”&nbsp;</p><p>“But she’s-”</p><p>“That’s a temporary problem,” Lachlan replied. “The court will rule in your favor, Tessa. It’s just a matter of time and <em>waiting</em>. You know that.”</p><p><em>I don’t know that.</em></p><p>“Right now, that’s not what I’m most worried about.”</p><p>“You can’t let yourself-”&nbsp;</p><p>“I can’t <em>help </em>it. I don’t <em>know </em>anything. No one will tell me <em>anything!</em>”&nbsp;</p><p>In one sentence, what remaining composure the Witch had broke. A pitiful sob escaped her lips. With it, her control over her tears was gone. She was gasping as she clenched her fists tighter. The bite of her fingernails into her palm didn’t begin to touch her agony. She felt Lachlan’s arms wrap tightly around her. Her hands gripped desperately at the back of his pressed blazer and wrinkled it. If she could, she would apologize.&nbsp;</p><p>Tessa had been allowed to stay with her sister while she was in labor. Though her relationship with Clara was fractured and broken after nearly seven months of constant fighting, she phoned Tessa the instant her water broke. At twenty-nine weeks, it was too early for the baby to be born. Clara didn’t seem phased by this knowledge, though everything inside Tessa was screaming. The nurses reassured her of statistics and probabilities for the child’s survival, but luck had <em>never </em>been on Tessa’s side.&nbsp;</p><p>Clara didn’t know of the paperwork Tessa had filed throughout Clara’s pregnancy, nor the lawyers she hired to fight her sister for custody of her niece. She had been advised to keep that to herself. While Clara screamed in pain through each contraction, Tessa played the part of the caring sister and held her hand, though she felt no pity for the woman in the bed. From the moment Clara announced her pregnancy, Tessa’s allegiance was no longer with her, but with the child she would fight like hell to give a better life.&nbsp;</p><p><em>“It’s a girl,” Tessa’s favorite nurse, Stacy, told her as she squeezed her arms and blocked Tessa from going to the child.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>Tessa was aware of the silence in the room where there should have been screaming. There should have been crying. There should have been cooing. Instead, there was only the hushed voices of the doctors as they worked huddled in a circle that blocked Tessa’s view of her.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“A girl?” Tessa didn’t even know she was crying, but she tasted tears.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“She’s not breathing on her own. Tessa, we’re doing everything we can,” Stacy murmured. Her voice was calm, but it brought her no comfort. She squeezed her arms again, and Tessa wanted to fall into them.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Can I see-?”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“No,” Stacy’s voice was soft as she shook her head. “The police are here with Children’s Division. With the amount of drugs in her system, and in the baby’s, they’re taking her into state care.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“What?” Clara gasped out behind her. “Absolutely not!”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Ma’am-” Stacy began, but Clara cut her off.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“No. You’re not taking my daughter! My sister is a lawyer, she’ll-”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“I’m a foster parent,” Tessa said with a confidence she didn’t feel. Her eyes were on Stacy’s. She watched the nurse seem to soften. She watched the allegiance shift to her rather than the addict on the bed. “I’ve already filed papers. I want her.”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“Theresa!” Clara shouted. “You can’t-”&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>“I can,” Tessa replied, but she didn’t look at Clara. She didn’t dare. “And I will.”&nbsp;</em></p><p>Even though Tessa’s wishes were shared with the team of social workers and police officers, everything was moving in slow motion. Percy had driven her to the courthouse to officially file the papers she needed just hours after the birth, but it would take time for the wheels to officially move forward, <em>if </em>they fell in her favor at all. As a lawyer herself, she understood the hoops and the steps that would need to be taken. Tessa had been prepared for this part, or at least, she <em>thought </em>she was prepared.&nbsp;</p><p>She had anticipated the NICU, particularly with Clara’s heavy drug use.&nbsp;</p><p>She hadn’t been prepared for her niece to not be breathing on her own.&nbsp;</p><p>No amount of preparation could have prepared Tessa for the agony of hours in the NICU waiting room with the doctors and nurses legally unable to tell her anything. Even though she had filed paperwork, with the baby in state custody, Tessa had no legal right to the information. At least, not yet.&nbsp;</p><p>It was torture.&nbsp;</p><p>Thirty-two hours of it.&nbsp;</p><p>“Tessa, look at me,” Lachlan said softly.&nbsp;</p><p>His arms were secure around her. His hand was rubbing circles on Tessa’s back. It didn’t bring her real comfort, nothing did, but it was close. It brought her as much as when Percy forced her to lay her head on her shoulder and kissed the top of her head, or when Serenity squeezed her hand and sang Italian lullabies in her ear.</p><p>Prior to two days ago, Tessa hadn’t realized how many people actually cared about her. After a decade in foster care and another decade of stubborn self-imposed exile, the Witch still had trouble believing her friends were really there, but they showed up in waves. Percy and Shiloh, despite their own three month old at home, had made frequent trips to the hospital. Serenity left only to sleep, but had spent the first night curled up beside Tessa. Lachlan was the same.&nbsp;</p><p>Celeste, her High Priestess, brought her pumpkin soup made especially for her by her girlfriend, Cordelia, for lunch. Elyse and Piper Aldersley provided them all with dinner from Tessa’s favorite sandwich shop while Elyse, who owned the hospital, passed out forms to her friends to volunteer in the unit so they could see her while the court decided on Tessa’s fate. Jo Darcey, her mentor, had been the one she called at 3am when Serenity was sleeping and she couldn’t breathe through a panic attack.&nbsp;</p><p>She had people. Lots of them.&nbsp;</p><p>They just weren’t the ones she wanted right now.&nbsp;</p><p>She wanted her niece. Her <em>daughter</em>.&nbsp;</p><p>“Tessa. Look at me.”&nbsp;</p><p>Lachlan pulled Tessa back just enough so he could meet her eyes. She wasn’t crying anymore, but her vision remained blurry no matter how many times she blinked. She felt him reach up to wipe at her tears. She wanted to tell him not to waste the energy, because more would follow. Instead, the words that left her lips were ones that would make her laugh in any other circumstance.&nbsp;</p><p>“I wrinkled your jacket.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Who fucking cares,” Lachlan murmured. “It’s just a jacket.”&nbsp;</p><p>“But-”&nbsp;</p><p>“I’m going to promise you something,” He said softly. His eyes didn’t leave hers. “Next year, on September 15th, the day between our birthdays, I’m going to do something. I’m going to come pick up your daughter, and I’m going to go take her to do something fun. Ice cream, cake, or anything we can think of-”&nbsp;</p><p>“Lachl-” Tessa began, but he kept talking over her.&nbsp;</p><p>“And I am going to do that <em>every</em> year for as long as she’ll tolerate it. Even when she’s fourteen and she thinks I’m awful, I’m going to pick her up from school and we’ll go get ice cream and celebrate our birthdays together. When she’s twenty-one and at Yale, Harvard, or any other brilliant, incredible school, I’ll get on a plane to take her to ice cream.”&nbsp;</p><p>“But we don’t know if-” She began. He didn’t let her finish.&nbsp;</p><p><em>We don’t know if she’ll survive this.&nbsp;</em></p><p>“She will,” Lachlan said firmly. “She’s fire, like you. She’ll pull through this, and then she’ll go home with you. She’ll grow up to be just as strong, fierce, and wonderful as you, Tess.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Thank you,” Tessa whispered. She squeezed her eyes shut as she steadied her breathing. “I love you.”&nbsp;</p><p>“I love you too.” He leaned forward to press a kiss to her forehead. “Do you have a name yet?”&nbsp;</p><p>“She’s not-”&nbsp;</p><p>“Fuck the court right now, Tess. Even if the name is just for you. What’s her name?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Mia.”&nbsp;</p><p>The word came out soft, surprising even Tessa. Over the past seven months, she had poured over baby name books in the hope that one would jump out at her. In the end, she had resolved to wait until she met her. Her name, Tessa had decided, had to suit her. How could she name someone she hadn’t met?&nbsp;</p><p>“Mia Laine,” Tessa repeated, this time a little stronger. “Laney, for short. Mia… it means… it means <em>mine</em>.”&nbsp;</p><p>“The perfect name.”&nbsp;</p><p>Lachlan put his arm around her and helped her to her feet. Tessa was exhausted. She wanted to sink back into the chair, but he held her up and guided her over to the window that was her only possibility of laying eyes on her daughter. She wanted to be inside. She didn’t want her to be alone.&nbsp;</p><p>“Wait here, okay?” Lachlan whispered before he slipped away from her.&nbsp;</p><p>Tessa watched with envious eyes as he went to the door of the NICU. Elyse Aldersley had pulled many strings to allow her friends to be volunteers, but the decision had been made to not extend that privilege to Tessa. Logically, she understood the need to make it look like she was following <em>every </em>rule. Still, she ached to be inside like he was.</p><p>Tessa didn’t stop watching Lachlan as he spoke to the nurses. Her brows furrowed as he motioned to her. Realization of what he was doing bloomed in her chest when the nurses began to move the tiny crib from the far side of the room, where it had been the past two days, closer to the window.&nbsp;</p><p>Tessa’s fingers pressed tightly against the glass as she finally laid eyes on <em>her</em>. She couldn’t touch her, but she <em>could </em>see the steady rise and fall of the baby’s chest. She could see her tiny balled fists, her beautiful pink skin, and the ten perfect toes. She was perfect, and she was <em>hers</em>.&nbsp;</p><p>“Laney,” Tessa whispered, her fingers curling against the glass.&nbsp;</p><p>She watched Lachlan sit in the chair next to Laney’s crib. She was impossibly jealous of the way he was able to reach into the gloved sides of the crib to lightly brush his finger against her tiny hand. She watched Laney’s tiny hand curl around his finger. Though she couldn’t hear him, she knew him well enough to read his lips and understand the words he spoke to her daughter.&nbsp;</p><p>“Look,” He said with a motion to the window where Tessa was desperately pressed. “Laney, that’s your Mommy. She’s fighting for you. She loves you so, so much.”&nbsp;</p><p><em>I am, Laney.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>I’m fighting for you. I’ll never stop.&nbsp;</em></p><p><em>I love you so much.</em></p>
