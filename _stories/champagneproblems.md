<blockquote class="quote-alt">
  <p>December 20th, 2019</p>
  <p><strong>Piper</strong></p>
</blockquote>

<p>“One of these days, you’re going to drop salsa onto that pretty dress of yours, you know.”</p>

<p>Riley’s voice brought Piper’s attention from her attempt to open the packet of hot sauce with her teeth. As she sat in the passenger seat of her best friend’s Jeep, an unwrapped burrito was carefully balanced in her hand. The only thing protecting her ball gown was a handful of napkins strategically tucked into the sweetheart neckline and scattered across her lap.</p>

<p>“If that happened, which it won’t, then,” Piper began as she tore open the packet and squeezed the hot sauce onto her food. “I would make your ass drive me home and change, but that, fortunately, has never happened.”</p>

<p>“Never say <em>never,</em> Pipes.”</p>

<p>“Please,” Piper rolled her eyes. She took a bite and sighed happily at the taste. As she chewed, she held her best friend’s gaze. When she finally swallowed, she continued. “Us Aldersleys are trained in how to eat in a ballgown before we can even walk.”</p>

<p>“You know, with your mom, that truly wouldn’t surprise me.”</p>

<p>“If I were you, I’d be more worried about you being the one to spill on <em>your</em> dress.”</p>

<p>“Please,” She laughed. “I’m way better at this than you will ever be.”</p>

<p>They were at a small park near the massive event hall that hosted the Aldersley Children’s Foundation’s Christmas Ball. As was typical for the two of them before any event, Riley had been the one to pick her up. Piper felt significantly more comfortable arriving with her than with any driver.</p>

<p>Riley shifted her car into park as she pulled into a spot near the playground. This was, without a doubt, Piper’s favorite event of the year. Every aspect of the glamorous Christmas Ball was for charity. From the seats, the auction, and the entertainment, every last penny went towards benefiting the lives of supernatural children both in Greenborough and across the country. Piper couldn’t be more proud to be a part of both the foundation <em>and</em> the Aldersley family.</p>

<p>Even <em>with</em> the knowledge of all the good things that would come of the money raised tonight, Piper desperately needed the pregame burritos.</p>

<p>Shortly after Riley received her driver’s licence, their tradition began. With her own set of wheels, Riley insisted they would no longer arrive at events with professional drivers. Instead, they would arrive together, in her Jeep, but first, they’d feed themselves something disgusting. As an Aldersley, eyes would be on Piper from the moment she arrived until the lights went out. Riley would have similar attention, too, though the press never preyed on her in quite the same way. Like always, she helped her to feel a little more prepared by letting her be a typical eighteen year old for an hour, even if she <em>was</em> eating burritos in a ballgown.</p>

<p>They fell silent as they ate their burritos. Though they joked about it, they were each careful to not let a drop of anything spill on their dresses. Each bite was meticulous. In the end, they were both spotless and better prepared for the event. For Piper, it would be hours of socializing, answering dozens of questions about her reading program, Reading By The Stars, and posing for photos, many of which would be with her boyfriend, Noah Huntington.</p>

<p>After three and a half years of dating, the media was getting restless for the “next steps” in her relationship. In many ways, Piper knew that Noah was, as well. Piper, on the other hand, wasn’t so sure. Things had been simple and happy while they were in high school, but things had changed when university acceptance letters came pouring in. Piper got accepted to the prestigious Greenborough University, and he did not. As it was, he was attending school nearly five hours away at the University of Portland. Piper gushed often about how much she enjoyed her studies, while he openly hated college.</p>

<p>Piper, too, had gotten in to the University of Portland.</p>

<p><em>“You’re going, right?” Noah asked when they received their letters. His green eyes were focused on hers. In her hands, she held her letter to Greenborough University. Her other acceptance letters - Harvard, Yale, and Princeton - were upstairs on her desk with the letter from the University of Portland.</em></p>

<p><em>“To GU? Of cour-”</em></p>

<p><em>“No, babe, to Portland. With me.”</em></p>

<p><em>Her eyes immediately left Noah to find Riley, who was sitting on the couch in her living room. She, too, looked absolutely stunned at the question. One glance was all it took. Riley sat up and took her side as she always did whenever Piper couldn’t find her own words. Riley always had her back, endlessly and forever.</em></p>

<p><em>“Noah, no offense, I’m sure Portland is a fine school, but you can’t seriously be asking her to choose Portland over Greenborough. The Women’s Studies Department, which Piper just got accepted to, is not only harder to get into than the Ivies, but ranked higher too. You know, the Ivies that she also got accepted to. Even if Greenborough wasn’t her top choice, do you really think she’d give up all those other amazing sch-”</em></p>

<p><em>“Last time I checked, this was a discussion between me and my girlfriend, I don’t know why you always think you’re a third part in our relationship, Riley.” Noah’s tone was sharp. “If you wanted to date her you-”</em></p>

<p><em>“Enough,” Piper said sharply. “Don’t talk to her like that.”</em></p>

<p><em>“Piper-”</em></p>

<p><em>“Maybe you didn’t ask for my opinion,” Riley stated, crossing her arms over her chest. “But, if you were a good boyfriend, you’d be happy for her instead of bitching about her choice.”</em></p>

<p><em>“I’m choosing Greenborough,” Piper sighed, shaking her head. A happy moment had turned into a sour one, as many with him had quickly began to. “End of discussion.”</em></p>

<p><em>“I should have known,” Noah grumbled. He looked between the two of them as he picked up his coat. “Of course you’d stay near her.”</em></p>

<p>In the months they’d been apart, things had been tense. Riley, just like every member of her family and friends, reassured her that she would have been an idiot to give up her spot in the Greenborough University Class of 2023. She knew that, and she knew Noah knew it too. It didn’t stop him from bringing up their distance in <em>every</em> conversation they had, even if he came home to visit nearly every other weekend.</p>

<p>“You okay, Pipes?”</p>

<p>“Hm?” Piper lifted her eyes to find hers. Their food was finished, and it was time to head to the event. She should be happy, but a knot of dread had formed instead in her stomach. “Yeah. Just… thinking about…”</p>

<p>“Noah?” Riley continued when she trailed off. Piper nodded.</p>

<p>“Lunch was <em>weird</em>, right?”</p>

<p>Noah, back in town for Christmas break, had been the one to insist on going to lunch with Hendrix and Riley. While neither of her best friends were the biggest fans of her boyfriend, they both agreed to go and be civil. Piper had hoped, desperately, that something would spark in their face-to-face connection. She hoped that seeing him would ease the dread that came from every phone conversation, text, and video call.</p>

<p><em>“I got into UCLA!” Noah exclaimed before he even took off his coat. “I’m moving to LA in January.”</em></p>

<p><em>“Wow that’s… great, Noah,” Piper stated slowly, clearly stunned by his introduction. Despite her initial shock, she was happy for him. “When did you find out?”</em></p>

<p><em>“Two weeks ago,” Noah’s tone was defensive as he sat next to her. Across the table, Riley and Hendrix were giving their own murmurs of congratulations. “It’s a great school, Piper.”</em></p>

<p><em>“I never said it wasn’t.”</em></p>

<p><em>“I mean we can’t all have an easy ride into Gre-”</em></p>

<p><em>“I’m going to, on her behalf, stop you right there,” Hendrix said sharply. “It was Piper’s brain that got her into GU, not whatever the end of your sentence was about to be.”</em></p>

<p><em>“I know,” Noah replied sharply.</em></p>

<p><em>“Do you now?” Riley added, which was met with a sigh from her boyfriend.</em></p>

<p><em>His arm moved to settle around her shoulders. It felt heavy and it took everything in her to not pull away. Once, his touch had filled her with warmth and joy. She wished those feelings would come back. She wished it was easy again.</em></p>

<p><em>“I know Piper is fucking brilliant. I was just saying…”</em></p>

<p><em>His gaze turned to her. His green eyes lit up as he smiled. She knew that smile. For a moment, it made her melt. At least until his next words came, and her stomach sank.</em></p>

<p><em>“I was hoping you’d move to LA with me.”</em></p>

<p>Piper hadn’t known what to say. In the end, she had switched the conversation with a reassurance that she would <em>think</em> about it. She wouldn’t. She couldn’t. UCLA, while an incredible school, wasn’t where she wanted to be. She wanted to be here. Throughout lunch, Piper was aware that he was unhappy with her. She explained her avoidance with nerves for the benefit, where he was meant to be her date.</p>

<p>“It was weird,” Riley replied cautiously. Piper knew that tone. Though she had her own opinion, she was being careful until she knew, fully, how Piper felt about it. “Are you going to do it?”</p>

<p>“No.”</p>

<p>“Good.”</p>

<p>“I’m…” Piper hesitated, reaching up to run her fingers through her hair before she realized it was carefully pinned back into place. She dropped her hand to her lap and turned her eyes to meet Riley’s.</p>

<p>“I’m going to break up with him.”</p>

<p>“Good. When?” Riley asked. Her gaze was neutral, but Piper knew the smile of approval hidden underneath.</p>

<p>“Tomorrow, I don’t want the focus of tonight to be on me or my relationship. At least, not anymore than it already will be.”</p>

<p>“Fair enough,” She gently nudged her before she leaned over to put her arm around Piper’s shoulders. She leaned into her best friend and smiled when the gentle kiss was pressed to the top of her head. “Remember, if you need out of <em>any</em> conversation tonight, just tug on your ear.”</p>

<p>Piper chuckled at the mention of their signal. She hadn’t used it in years, but remembered it well. They had been children when Piper declared a tug on the ear meant they would be rescued from any situation, whether it be a boring conversation or backup with a parent. They used it a handful of times, and every time, help was immediately given between the two of them.</p>

<p>“A tug of the ear,” Piper replied with a smile as she tugged on her earlobe to solidify the gesture. “I love you, Ri.”</p>

<p>“Don’t go getting sappy on me now, Aldersley,” Riley chuckled.</p>

<p>“Let me love you, you bitch.”</p>

<p>“Never. It’s good for you to have to work for it.”</p>

<p>Piper laughed before she sat up and helped her to gather the wrappers of their burritos. Once they were carefully placed in the bag, Riley set it in the backseat. Without a word, she pulled back on the road and navigated them to the event hall.</p>

<p>The streets around the venue were lined with photographers and press. Still a block away, Piper took a moment to flip the visor down. Her hair was still carefully pinned into an elegant updo that showed off the earrings that were an early Christmas present from Jo: diamond stars that glittered when she moved her head. A matching necklace twinkled around her neck, a gift from her mother. Her dress, teal and decorated with shimmering crystals, was spotless. She looked perfect, though her stomach twisted.</p>

<p>When they pulled up at the curb, Riley stepped out of the car before she could move. As always, she beat the valet to open her door. Sometimes, Piper could understand the rumors that raged in the media that insisted she was in love with her best friend. Sometimes, they certainly <em>acted</em> like a couple. It was anything but the truth. She leaned on Riley throughout her life as a guiding light, but she was family. Riley was her constant, and she would be lost without her.</p>

<p>“You ready?” She asked, and she nodded and took her hand to step out of the car.</p>

<p>“<em>Piper!</em>”</p>

<p>Piper wasn’t sure which of the many photographers had called her name. Several of them, she presumed. Her immediate sight was a flurry of camera flashes that turned her vision into blinded dots. Still, a smile erupted on Piper’s face. It was poised and practiced, but she posed for their photo nonetheless. She was mindful. If she posed for too long, they’d call her a diva. If she didn’t pose long enough, she was a bitch. She knew the appropriate amount, as did Riley, and she felt her hand on her arm to guide her into the lobby of the building.</p>

<p>She was shivering when the heat hit them. Her coat was left in the car. No one wanted to see her puffy jacket on red carpet photos. They wanted to see her smile and pretty dress. They wanted to see a <em>princess</em>. Even if her mother no longer held the title of Fae Queen, her family’s status in the city meant that Piper was <em>forever</em> labeled as the city’s princess. Despite her accomplishments, that was who they expected her to be. She would smile and play the part.</p>

<p>“You alright?” Riley asked, stepping up to rub her arms in an attempt to warm her.</p>

<p>“Yeah, just trying to recover my sense of feeling in my arms and getting the camera flashes out of my eyes.”</p>

<p>“I thought you <em>liked</em> stars, Aldersley.”</p>

<p>“Yes, in the sky. Not in my retinas,” Piper laughed and gave her arms a squeeze.</p>

<p>“You’re so picky. <em>Such</em> a diva,” Riley teased. Her nose wrinkled as she burst into a smile. Within a few seconds, it faded as another body appeared at their side. It took several blinks for Noah’s form to come into focus.</p>

<p>“Hey, I assumed we were meeting outside,” Noah sighed. He was frowning. Already, Piper was ready for tonight to be over.</p>

<p>“In case you weren’t aware, Noah, it’s <em>freezing</em> outside. I don’t know if you’ve ever posed in December weather in a ballgown, but it tends to make people cold. Of <em>course</em> we were coming inside.”</p>

<p>“Nice to see you, too, Riley,” Noah muttered.</p>

<p>“A real pleasure,” Riley agreed in a tone that said she thought having a root canal would be more desirable. It took everything in Piper to not laugh.</p>

<p>Noah ignored Riley. His hand came to rest on the small of Piper’s back as he stepped in closer. She could smell his cologne, the same as always, and for a moment she let herself take in the comfort of the scent. It used to be one of her favorite things. While she still loved the way he smelled, his presence no longer lifted her up, but instead made her feel like she was drowning.</p>

<p>Piper glanced up, expecting him to lean down to kiss her, but instead his eyes were on Riley. The two were staring at each other and they <em>both</em> looked annoyed. Riley, she knew, was annoyed simply from the passive aggressive tone in Noah’s voice. Noah was bothered by her arrival with Riley. By now, he thought Piper should arrive with him. He made that opinion known as often as he could.</p>

<p>“I just figured we would take pictures together.”</p>

<p>“There will be plenty of time for photos,” Piper commented. She brushed a stray lock of her hair behind her ear. Her eyes scanned the lobby for her sister or her mother, who she knew would be busy inside running the event. She wondered if they needed help, or if she could somehow get away with hiding for the entire night.</p>

<p>“I know, but-”</p>

<p>“Hey Pipes. Hey Ri,” Hendrix’s voice cut off Noah as he stepped around a man to move over to their group. His bright grin made a small smile appear on her own lips. He stepped up beside her and leaned down to press a kiss to her cheek, then to Riley’s. “Piper, you look <em>beautiful.</em> Riley, you clean up alright.<em>”</em></p>

<p>“Please, you know I look better than you ever could,” Riley teased. At Hendrix’s presence, her mood was slightly better.</p>

<p>“What our feral friend means is <em>thank you</em>,” Piper teased as she looked to her other best friend. “You clean up nice yourself, Drix.”</p>

<p>Piper was aware that her <em>boyfriend</em> should have made some sort of comment on her appearance or <em>anything</em> about her. She wasn’t looking for him to tell her she was beautiful or shower her with compliments, but <em>one</em> moment where he wasn’t irritated with her didn’t feel like too much to ask. Was it asking <em>so</em> much to simply want him to be <em>happy</em> to see her?</p>

<p>“We should go fully inside,” She commented as she stepped towards the grand ballroom.</p>

<p>Piper didn’t wait for Noah to take her arm or even offer. Instead, she felt both Riley and Hendrix fall in to step beside her. Noah was behind them. She could feel his irritation in every step she took. If there was any part of her still holding out hope to not want to end their relationship, it was quickly failing.</p>

<p><em>Tomorrow, Piper. Just get through tonight, and you’ll be alright.</em></p>

<p>Tomorrow couldn’t come soon enough. The instant they were in the ballroom, Piper took a moment to marvel at the splendor of it all. The benefits thrown by the foundation were always beautiful, but her mother truly went above and beyond when it came to the Christmas celebration. Twinkling lights shimmered and cast the room in a warm glow. In corners, Christmas trees towered tall with silver and red ornaments decorated around the garland. It looked like a fairy tale ball, and Piper was a princess.</p>

<p>She knew the dancing on the ballroom floor would come after dinner, but she wanted the magic of those moments <em>now.</em> She remembered the first ball she attended with Noah three years ago. Dancing with him under the lights felt more magical than homecoming or prom would ever be. Back then, Piper thought she was in love. Maybe she was, but had just fallen out of it with him. Maybe he was <em>never</em> the right person for her.</p>

<p>The night was quickly becoming a haze. At some point, Riley and Hendrix had slipped off to converse with other members of their friends and family. Noah remained at her side. The absence of her best friends seemed to relieve some of his tension, though he had still barely acknowledged her at all.</p>

<p>“Piper, can I have a moment?”</p>

<p>She glanced up to see one of the journalists had stepped towards her. His face was kind as he held his voice recorder in his hand. Typically, she may have turned down any sort of interview without her sister’s guidance, but for now, she was happy to have something to pull her from her thoughts.</p>

<p>“Of course,” Piper replied with the warm, public-ready smile she used in these sort of situations. Noah turned and slid his arm around her waist to draw her in to his side. Very aware of the reporter’s careful eyes, she leaned into his touch. “I’m sorry, I’m afraid I don’t know your name.”</p>

<p>“Shaun Livingston, with the Greenborough Inquirer,” He replied with the small smirk that Piper knew meant he was watching her every movement. “I was hoping to have a moment to ask how your program, Reading By The Stars, is going. You’ve officially taken over as the Program Director in addition to attending the esteemed Greenborough University, correct? How is that going for you?”</p>

<p>“Amazing,” Piper stated as her smile brightened into something much more genuine, as it always did when she spoke of the program she dreamed up when she was fifteen. Being too young to <em>officially</em> take the reins, her mother and sister ran the program in her name for the past few years. Now that she was old enough, it was <em>hers</em>. “As of May, we have our program in every school in Greenborough, in addition to several branches of the library. We’ve started to push into nearby cities, as well.”</p>

<p>“That’s incredible. My daughter had a reading buddy last year and adored her,” He stated.</p>

<p>“I hope she continues to. Empowering children to read is my biggest passion, and Reading By The Stars allows me to do that.”</p>

<p>“How is school going for you? Are you loving Greenborough University?”</p>

<p>“I am,” Piper nodded. She never knew what to say when people asked her about school. She could sing praises about her studies for centuries. It was impossible to narrow it down. “Having the opportunity to-”</p>

<p>“To explore <em>other</em> educational settings is a dream of Piper’s,” Noah interjected. She felt his hand press against the small of her back.</p>

<p>“Oh? And what does that mean?” Shaun’s smile was devilish as he looked between them. Piper tried not to visibly tense.</p>

<p>“Oh noth-”</p>

<p>“Being apart this semester has been difficult for us,” Noah replied. One arm pulled her closer while his free hand ran through his brunette curls. “We’ve <em>both</em> looked in to other educational opportunities that might allow us to be closer and further our relationship.”</p>

<p>“Oh? Tell me more.”</p>

<p>“I would rather wait,” Piper stated before Noah could say anything else. Her hand gripped at his arm, nails biting slightly into his suit jacket. She hoped, desperately, that she was able to keep her irritation out of her voice. “We haven’t made any official decisions. I see my mother. If you’ll excuse us.”</p>

<p>Before either of them could say anything, she turned and pulled Noah away from the journalist. She guided him across the ballroom far away from the listening ears of Shaun Livingston and the Greenborough Inquirer. With each step, her irritation grew. There were few privacies allowed in Piper’s life, and she had no desire for rumors to spread about her education or her relationship.</p>

<p>“What <em>was</em> that?” Piper sighed when they were far enough away to be able to talk privately.</p>

<p>“What was what?” Noah asked with an innocent bat of his eyelashes over the green eyes that had once drawn her in. “We talked about this-”</p>

<p>“And I told you I wanted to think about it.”</p>

<p>“What is there to think about, Piper? What kind of future do we have if we’re hours apart?”</p>

<p><em>We don’t have a future, Noah.</em></p>

<p>“I’m in school, Noah.”</p>

<p>“You can go to school in LA.”</p>

<p><em>I don’t want to go to school in LA.</em></p>

<p>“I haven’t applied to UCLA.”</p>

<p>“You can apply. With your family’s name-”</p>

<p>“My family’s name isn’t how I want to get an education, Noah,” Piper’s tone was far sharper than she typically used with him. She saw the flicker of surprise in his eyes before he shook his head to hide it. “I know you seem to think I got into Greenborough University simply for being an Aldersley, but that isn’t true. I had a nearly perfect score on my SATs, and I had straight A’s throughout high school. Both of which you know-”</p>

<p>“And your… whatever Jo Darcey is to your mother is in charge of your program. Don’t you think that had some sort of play?”</p>

<p>“No. I don’t.”</p>

<p>“You can’t be serious,” Noah ran a hand through his hair as he frowned. “You can’t honestly think that had nothing to do with it.”</p>

<p>“I am serious, actually. If you think Jo Darcey would let me in to her program without earning it-”</p>

<p>“Please, Piper. That’s <em>exactly</em> what happened.”</p>

<p>“Will you stop interrupting me?” Piper snapped.</p>

<p>She crossed her arms over her chest for a split second before she remembered where they were. Immediately, she straightened her posture and let her arms fall to her side. She hoped she looked relaxed, but she had a feeling there would be a dozen articles about this moment tomorrow.</p>

<p>“I have no interest in you speaking for me, in front of press or otherwise, Noah.”</p>

<p>“I thought we were a <em>team,</em> Piper,” Noah sighed. His words were twinged with irritation, though he put on that same big eyed, puppy dog pout that he always did when he wasn’t getting his way about something. How many times had it worked?</p>

<p>“When you actually let me have a say in our future, we are.”</p>

<p>“Piper…” Noah ran his fingers through her hair. He studied her, and his expression told her one thing: he didn’t like what he was seeing. “You’ve changed.”</p>

<p>“I have,” Piper replied, putting the practiced smile back on her face as other partygoers filtered in closer to them. “I’m stronger.”</p>

<p>Piper slipped away from him under the guise of heading towards the bathrooms. It was the only place she could think where he wouldn’t follow her. Even then, it was only a matter of minutes until dinner. Though she knew she was seated at the same table as Hendrix and Riley, she also knew Noah would be at her side. She turned to scan the room just before she slipped to the back hallway. She wanted to talk to either of them, but neither of them were close enough to capture their attention.</p>

<p>By the time she returned from the bathroom, where she had spent several minutes with her head pressed against the cool glass of the mirror to steady herself, they were being guided to tables. She joined Noah at the circular table near the front of the room. With eyes on them, he played his part. He smiled at her brightly and stood to pull out her chair. Before she sat, he pressed a kiss to her cheek.</p>

<p>“I’m sorry,” He whispered in her ear when they sat back down. “I really thought you’d be excited about LA.”</p>

<p>“Noah, can we <em>please</em> talk about it tomorrow?”</p>

<p>“I… of course. I just want us to enjoy tonight.”</p>

<p>“Me too.”</p>

<p>The conversation at the dinner table was joyous, though Piper couldn’t recall a single word of what she said or what was discussed. Riley and Hendrix made several attempts to make her laugh, and Piper was certain she smiled appropriately, though she knew <em>both</em> of them saw right through her fake smiles. She answered every question from Noah’s mother, Martha, though the instant she answered, she couldn’t recall what was asked in the first place.</p>

<p>“Are you alright, Pipes?”</p>

<p>Hendrix’s voice caught her attention. For a moment, she let the fake smile falter before it was right back on her face. She knew he caught it. She glanced around the table to the empty dessert plates of her companions. She had apparently been poking at her tiramisu with her fork rather than take a single bite.</p>

<p>“What? Oh,” Piper shook her head to clear her thoughts. “Yes, just full, I think.”</p>

<p>“You always have room for dessert,” Hendrix said, tilting his head as he observed her.</p>

<p>“Maybe,” Martha replied from across the table. Piper locked eyes with her as she lifted her wine glass to her lips with a smirk. “Piper is making sure she can fit into a wedding dress.”</p>

<p>Her lips parted, though no sound came from her lips. For once, Martha Huntington had rendered her completely speechless. She had no idea how to, at least not in any capacity that <em>wasn’t</em> rude, tell her that she would rather eat a thousand tiramisus than put on a wedding dress, especially if her groom was the sour man at her side.</p>

<p>“You know, Martha,” Riley replied. Her tone was polite and cordial, but Piper knew her well enough to know the anger boiling under the surface. “If I were Piper, I’d take my chances and eat the tiramisu. In fact, I’d lick the plate clean and ask for seconds.”</p>

<p>“Same,” Hendrix agreed.</p>

<p>Piper considered doing just that. She could imagine the horrified look on Martha’s face as she let her tongue lick every last crumb from the fine china. She thought about eating every last piece of tiramisu on the planet while she informed Martha Huntington that her son was the <em>last</em> person in the world she wanted to marry. If she were anything but an Aldersley, she might have. Instead, she remained silent and pushed her plate away, no longer hungry.</p>

<p><em>You can get through this, Piper.</em></p>

<p><em>Smile for the party.</em></p>

<p><em>Then you can end it tomorrow.</em></p>

<p>Once plates were cleared, Noah stood up and held a hand out to her. That singular gesture brought her back to the first time they came to the benefit together. Back then, she had presumed him a fairy tale. As she looked at him tonight, she wished she could still see the dark haired, green eyed boy who had once been her prince instead of the entitled man who would ridicule her before meeting her in any sort of compromise.</p>

<p>“Do you want to dance?”</p>

<p><em>No, I want to be away from you.</em></p>

<p>Eyes were on her, so Piper let her face brighten into a smile. It didn’t meet her eyes, but she knew it would convince anyone near her, save for Hendrix and Riley. She knew they saw through every fake smile and polite word. As much as she wanted to, she couldn’t refuse him now, so instead she took his hand and let him help her to her feet.</p>

<p>“I’d love to.”</p>

<p>On the dance floor, she felt his arms slip around her. She let her arms wrap around his neck as she rested her head against his chest. In this position, she could hear the steady thudding of his heart. Once, she’d considered it a melody just for her. She closed her eyes and let herself move with him. If she focused, she could slip away and enjoy her night. Maybe Hendrix or Riley would cut in for a dance and find a way to, somehow, make her laugh.</p>

<p>Piper was deep in her own thoughts. She didn’t notice he had pulled her over towards the band, nor how it had quieted. She felt eyes on her. They cut through her like knives as she watched one of them hand a microphone to Noah. In that instant, her stomach sank.</p>

<p><em>No. No, Noah. What are you doing? Noah. Please.</em></p>

<p>“If I could have everyone’s attention, please?”</p>

<p>His voice seemed impossibly loud as he spoke into the mic, though she was certain that was due to the pounding in her own ears at the way everyone was turning to watch them. The dance floor seemed to part as couples stepped to the side to give those further back a view at what was occurring. She didn’t know what was coming next, not exactly, but her gut instinct told her to run.</p>

<p>She looked around the room, trying to find sight of anyone that might save her. Across the room, her eyes fell on Riley. Seconds before, she was twirling Bailey around. Now, she was tense. Beside her, Hendrix had been dancing with Sydney. Both of them looked ready to rush to her rescue, but before she could say a word, Noah continued.</p>

<p>“In case you don’t know me, my name is Noah Huntington. Over the last three and a half years, I’ve had the pleasure of getting to call Piper Aldersley my girlfriend…”</p>

<p><em>Oh. Oh, fuck.</em></p>

<p>“If you don’t have the honor of knowing this beautiful girl, let me genuinely tell you that you’re missing out on one of the most incredible people you’ll ever meet. Every day, I’m honored to say she’s mine.”</p>

<p>Around her, she heard the collective sighs. They thought the words were adorable. Months ago, she might have been falling for it too right along with them. Instead, she was frozen in wide eyed fear. She was an animal caught in a trap ready to chew her own leg off to flee.</p>

<p>“Piper.”</p>

<p>Noah’s green eyes fell on her as he stepped closer to her once more. He took her hand, and somehow she was smiling even though she was certain one of the <em>many</em> courses of her dinner, burrito included, were about to make a reappearance on his shoes.</p>

<p>“From the very moment that I met you, it seemed as if the very universe made us happen. For so long, I felt like you were completely out of my league. Sometimes, I think you still are.”</p>

<p>Piper heard the collective chuckle from the room. The noise sounded far away. In her ears, her heart pounded. Her chest felt tight, and she wished she could bring herself to look away from him. She wanted her mother, her sister, Jo, Riley, Hendrix, Saoirse… <em>anyone</em> else. Instead, she was hanging on his every word.</p>

<p>“Despite everything, you chose me. In the past three and a half years, you’ve made me believe in true love. You’d be surprised, growing up in Greenborough, but I didn’t believe in magic until the moment we shared our first kiss. With that…”</p>

<p><em>Please don’t, Noah.</em></p>

<p>Another collective gasp followed as Noah knelt on one knee in front of her. He slipped his hand into the pocket of his suit coat and produced a ring box. Her eyes didn’t leave his as he opened it and her gaze fell on the ring he was presenting her with.</p>

<p>In another world, she would have found it beautiful. She recognized it immediately. It was his great-great-grandmother’s ring, after all. It had been passed down the line of the Huntington family for generations. If she had paid attention to dinner at all, she might have noticed it missing from Martha Huntington’s left hand. Once upon a time, Piper had dreamed of the day it would be on her own finger. In that fantasy, she was older and he proposed to her in private, not in front of hundreds of people who seemed to be holding in a collective breath.</p>

<p>“I know we’re young, and I know there are people in this room that think we’d be insane to marry when we have so much time, but I can’t help but look to your sister and Shiloh. I see our love mirrored in them, and I know you’re the person I’m going to grow old with.”</p>

<p>Piper turned her head at the mention of her sister. She found Percy instantly as if she was drawn by a magnet. She was standing beside Shiloh with Alice in her arms. Her brother-in-law’s arm was around Percy’s waist like it belonged there. Percy looked concerned. When their eyes met, Piper swore she saw the <em>slightest</em> shake of her head, though she couldn’t be certain it wasn’t in her head.</p>

<p>“Piper Quinn Aldersley,” Noah’s voice pulled her eyes back to him. “Will you make me the happiest man in the world and be my wife?”</p>

<p>It was her turn to speak. She watched him hand the microphone off to a member of the band, who had stepped forward to collect it. Surely, they expected she would say yes and he would want both hands to hold her close. Instead, Piper was frozen. Every second ticked by like a thousand years. She was aware of <em>every</em> last pair of eyes on her. The flash of a camera caught her attention, though she didn’t look away from his green eyes.</p>

<p>“Piper, did you hear me?” Noah murmured under his breath. “This is the part where you say yes.”</p>

<p><em>But I don’t want to say yes.</em></p>

<p>“Piper.”</p>

<p><em>How could you put me on the spot like this? If you really loved me-</em></p>

<p>“Piper, do you really think they’re going to love you if you say no?”</p>

<p>The words were barely a whisper. They were meant only for her ears. In that moment, everything clicked into place. The conversation at lunch, the desire for photos outside, talking to the reporter, and now the very, very public proposal. He knew she was gone. <em>This</em> was his way of coming out ahead in the breakup. He knew there would be two outcomes to this moment: either she would say yes and he would have what he wanted <em>or</em> she would say no and he would be the heartbroken victim on one knee. He would either end the night engaged to an Aldersley or the poor soul who had his heart broken by one.</p>

<p>With that, Piper looked up to lock eyes with Riley.</p>

<p>And then she tugged on her ear.</p>
