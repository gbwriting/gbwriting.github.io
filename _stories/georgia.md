<blockquote data-alt="true" class="quote-alt"><strong>Nyx</strong></blockquote><p>“So, are you going to have that look on your face the whole fucking night, or are you going to party?”</p><p>The voice pulled Nyx’s attention from their current task, which had been a half-hearted attempt to separate their clean clothes from the dirty ones in their far too overstuffed duffel bag. Two nights ago, when the second one they’d been using split down the middle, any attempts of sorting went out the window. Now, they were stuck with a pile of clothing stuck in the eternal limbo between clean and dirty.</p><p>“Can you go bother someone else?” Nyx replied, shaking her head as she focused back on the task. He’d shut up eventually. He always did.</p><p>“You’ve been in a sour mood for two days,” Dylan commented.</p><p>The drummer pushed himself off the brick wall, where he’d been leaning as he smoked his cigarette. Or joint. Or whatever he decided to pull into his lungs in an attempt to look the part of the tortured rockstar. He’d picked up the habit three weeks ago, when they’d played their first show with Lumina and been consumed with whatever the fuck the other opening act had done before the show.</p><p>If Dylan wanted to spend his time with white lines in bathrooms, Nyx didn’t care. Not as long as he could still drum.</p><p>“I’ve been perfectly fucking pleasant.”</p><p>It was a lie and they both knew it.</p><p>None of them were happy with her right now, and Nyx wasn’t exactly thrilled with themself right now, either.</p><p>“Look, I don’t know what the fuck your deal is, but—”</p><p>“But what, Dylan?”</p><p>Nyx accompanied their words with a slam of the van’s back door. The used-but-new-to-them van quivered with the force. The whole band knew it wouldn’t last more than this tour. Nyx cursed herself for being rough with it. They didn’t need to <em>help </em>it’s end.</p><p>“But we all think you’re being a bitch about everything.”</p><p>“Oh, is that so? Tell me, Dyl. How am I being a <em>bitch?”</em></p><p>“Because we all want to play the festival, and you’re the one saying no like you’re the only one with a goddamn say.” </p><p>“Yeah, well, maybe I am.” </p><p>That wasn’t a fair statement, and Nyx damn well knew it. Echoed Skies was meant to be a democracy. Sure, it didn’t always break down that way, but in terms of things like <em>this</em>, it should be more of a vote. Nyx agreed that it should be, and yet… </p><p>And yet, they were the one being stubborn and putting their foot down.</p><p>Nyx was well aware of the potential consequences of saying no to the offer to play Peachtree Fest. Nyx <em>understood </em>that it was one of the quickest growing music festivals in the scene. If it had been anywhere else in the goddamn country, Nyx would have leapt at the opportunity, the rest of the band be damned. </p><p>But Peachtree Fest was in <em>Atlanta</em>. </p><p>Somewhere Nyx just wasn’t quite ready to go just yet. </p><p>“I said no, Dyl. No means no.” </p><p>“And what if the rest of us say yes?” Dylan countered, the cigarette falling from his hand to the pavement. Nyx’s eyes followed as his boot ground it into the concrete. </p><p>There it was. </p><p>The ultimatum. </p><p>They’d been waiting on it.</p><p>Nyx knew this was ridiculous. It was just a festival. They’d played plenty like it. It was <em>just </em>a city. It just… so happened to be the one where they’d grown up. In a park less than five minutes from the sprawling Victorian home where the person she loved most in the world had once lived. Where that person <em>didn’t </em>anymore, because they didn’t live <em>anywhere</em>. </p><p>Because they were <em>gone</em>. </p><p>Nyx had <em>lost </em>them.</p><p>What if the new owners had painted it a boring shade of brown, a stark contrast from the various hues of purple it had been throughout her life? Nyx couldn’t count the number of times she’d stood in the paint aisle of The Home Depot comparing shades of lavender, periwinkle, and plum. Nor could she begin to quantify the arguments between members of her family on whether or not mulberry, her grandmother’s favorite shade, was acceptable for a respectable Victorian. </p><p>As if Lydia Montgomery-Bryant gave a fuck about being *respectable*. </p><p>Nyx wasn’t ready to see it. </p><p>Not the house. Not the park. Not Atlanta. Not the whole fucking state.</p><p>“Then I guess you’re performing without a singer, aren’t you?” Nyx replied finally, blue eyes lifting to lock with Dylan’s darker brown ones. He stood beside her now, leering over her, but she refused to let him intimidate her. “Tell me how that goes for you.” </p><p>“Really? That’s your argument?” </p><p>“Yeah. It is. If going to the fucking festival is so important to you, then you’ll perform without me.” </p><p>“Seriously, Nyx?” Dylan blinked. He took a step back, holding his hands up defensively. In that one gesture, he looked like the man she’d first met. One who didn’t hide behind cliches of what he thought he should be. “Do you honestly think we’d just perform without you?” </p><p>Nyx didn’t answer. It was far more terrifying to admit that it was something they had been afraid of for days. Everyone was replaceable, even them. Sure, they wrote the songs that were quickly gaining traction, but what did that mean? </p><p>“Maybe if you told us <em>why</em>, Nyx,” Dylan countered, clearly not wanting to go down the road he was headed down with them. “Why are you saying no?” </p><p>Nyx couldn’t blame them. </p><p>As far as anyone else in the band knew, they were from Moreno Valley, California. They’d almost perfected the mask of their once <em>darlin</em><em>’</em> Southern accent. As far as they knew, Nyx had never even <em>been </em>to Atlanta, let alone have ghosts waiting there for them. </p><p>“Well, you know what they say. The Devil went down to Georgia…” </p><p>“Yeah,” Dylan said with a chuckle, one that was dry and humorless. He saw their deflection for what it was: a pitiful attempt of avoidance. “I think he was looking for a soul to steal, not partying at a music festival.”</p><p>“You know, maybe that’s where the good souls are,” Nyx shrugged, still not looking at him. “Too risky. You know how I get. One good fiddle and my soul is gone.” </p><p>“Like any of us think you have a soul to begin with, Nyx.”</p><p>“Touché.” </p><p>“Maybe for <em>this </em>instance, the Devil’s flight gets delayed.” </p><p>“Please, you know the Devil <em>drives. </em>Though, I suppose the ‘Highway to Hell’ could be rather congested.<em>” </em></p><p>“Nyx.” </p><p>“What? Don’t tell me you don’t know your classic rock, Dylan.” </p><p>“Nyx,” Dylan repeated, but they kept going.</p><p>“You know, I saw this quote once. Something about how the fact that there’s a highway to hell and a stairway to heaven—” </p><p>“<em>Nyx.</em>” </p><p>“Please.” </p><p>Nyx’s voice was quiet. Meek. They stared at the ground and ground the toe of their boot into the pavement. Anything to avoid looking at Dylan. Anything to avoid the topic. </p><p>“Please. I’m not ready to talk about it. Just… please.” </p><p>In front of her eyes, Dylan softened. Despite his rockstar, asshole persona, internally he was as soft and broken as the rest of them. He reached out and put his hands on her upper arms, holding just above their elbows. Nyx exhaled. </p><p>“Just please,” They continued, finally lifting their eyes to look at him. The look in his eyes made her feel vulnerable and raw, but they <em>needed </em>him to understand. “Back me up on this one.” </p><p>Nyx had no way of knowing how long he stared at her, completely wordless. It could have been a matter of seconds or it could have been hours, but their body was vibrating with anxiety in every passing moment. Finally, he let out a sigh. </p><p>“You have a good reason?” He asked. </p><p>“Yes.” </p><p>“Fine,” Dylan sighed, reaching up to push Nyx’s hair behind their ear. She closed her eyes at the touch. “Just know one thing, Nyx.” </p><p>Nyx sighed, expecting more of a lecture or even more of a guilt trip. </p><p>“We all love you,” He said instead. Her eyes opened to look at him, and he held her gaze. “And we’d all support you. When you’re ready, just talk to us.” </p><p>“I know,” Nyx murmured, swallowing down the lump in their throat.</p><p>“And one more thing,” Dylan continued. He let go of her arms to, instead, slide his arm around her shoulders. “Stay the fuck away from fiddles.” </p>
