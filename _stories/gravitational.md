<blockquote class="quote-alt"><p>August 31st, 2006</p><p><strong>Reagan</strong></p></blockquote><p>&nbsp;“I don’t get why we have to learn history dates. It’s history for a fucking reason.”&nbsp;</p><p>As Reagan was attempting to battle with the new, very stubborn combination to her locker, Dominic’s voice made her glance up. She smiled at the Warlock as he leaned against the nearby lockers and ran a hand through his dark hair.&nbsp;</p><p>Right… something? Left… nothing.&nbsp;</p><p>Considering it was nearly two weeks into school, it was <em>embarrassing </em>that she couldn’t remember.&nbsp;</p><p>“Still struggling with that thing?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Yes,” Reagan mumbled as she tugged on the lock to test the combination. As she expected, it didn’t budge. “And for your information, Mom says we learn history so we know how to keep from repeating the mistakes of our ancestors.”&nbsp;</p><p>“And what would your ancestors have said about your locker combination?”&nbsp;</p><p>“That it’s been two weeks of school and I should remember it by now-”&nbsp;</p><p>“No, Rae,” Dominic chuckled as he nudged her hand out of the way to grab ahold of the lock. She heard words murmured under his breath and then the unmistakable sound of the lock clicking open in his hand. “They would have reminded you that you’re a Witch, and you don’t have to remember a combination if you use magic.”&nbsp;</p><p>He smirked at her as he took a step back. She knew that expression well. It was one his mother always commented on. She could hear her voice now commenting on how that look could get Dominic away with murder. To Reagan, it just made her laugh.</p><p>“And <em>what </em>would you do without me?”&nbsp;</p><p>“I would dig the planner out from my bag where I have it written down,” Reagan replied immediately as she opened her locker and began to load the books she would need for her homework into her messenger bag. Beside her, Dominic put a hand over his heart and let out a mock gasp.&nbsp;</p><p>“You wound me, Rae.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Good. Your ego was getting a little too big for your own good.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Ha, very funny.”&nbsp;</p><p>“I’m hilarious,” Reagan replied as she shut her locker. Beside her, Dominic pushed himself off of the wall and straightened to his full height. His smirk was still on his lips, and she tilted her head as she watched him. “You look like you’re up to no good today.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Always. You ready to go?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Aren’t you forgetting something?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Not that I’m aware of, other than <em>all </em>of the dates that I learned in British History today.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Your sister.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Oh. Shit. I… forgot?”&nbsp;</p><p>Reagan crossed her arms over her chest as she watched him. It was nearly the end of the second week of school, and not one day had Dominic remembered that they were meant to collect his younger sister, Adria, from her kindergarten class to walk home with them.&nbsp;</p><p>Since it was only a ten minute walk from their school to the Eastern Coven Manor, their school insisted they didn’t need to ride the bus. When the weather turned cold, Dominic’s parents would alternate with Reagan’s mother to get the three of them to school. With the current, almost autumnal weather, the three of them were perfectly capable of walking.&nbsp;</p><p>“Well, it’s a good thing I always remember, isn’t it?”&nbsp;</p><p>“You’re too responsible for your own good, Rae.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Well, I <em>will </em>be High Priestess one day, won’t I?” Reagan commented.&nbsp;</p><p>She started down the hallway that separated the upper classes from the elementary school classrooms. Dominic fell into step at her side, his thumbs hooking in the straps of his backpack. She knew it had always been the two of them walking home from school, but she failed to see how he continued to forget. Even if it was a game to him, after nine days of it, she was failing to find it amusing any longer.</p><p>“Rae!”&nbsp;</p><p>The sound of Adria Benson’s tiny voice pulled Reagan from her thoughts. Adria bolted from the door of her classroom, down the hallway, and into Reagan’s arms. She had only a second of head start to kneel to the ground and capture the child into a hug. As was the routine <em>every </em>day she picked up the child from her classroom, Adria’s tiny arms wrapped tightly around her neck.&nbsp;</p><p>“Hey Jellybean,” Reagan grinned and kissed the child’s cheek.&nbsp;</p><p>She held her for a moment before she pulled back, and Adria’s face spread out in a wild smile. Just a week before school started, Adria had lost one of her front teeth. Now, when she smiled, the gap in her teeth was prominent. Especially since she had a tendency to stick her tongue through the hole. Though she knew it wouldn’t happen, Reagan secretly wished her tooth would never grow back and she could have this giddy grin forever.&nbsp;</p><p>“Learn anything cool today?”&nbsp;</p><p>“No,” Adria sighed one of her large, overdramatic huffs that always made Reagan giggle. “Miss Cunningham read a book about lions. I wanted to learn about <em>sharks</em>.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Maybe tomorrow she’ll read a book about sharks?”</p><p>“Probably not,” Adria crossed her arms over her chest. The gesture made the The <em>Little Mermaid </em>backpack that was too large for her tiny frame wobble. “Kindergarten is <em>boring, </em>we don’t learn about anything cool.”&nbsp;</p><p>“You could be like your boring brother. He had to study history today. He thought it was pretty boring, too.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Do <em>you </em>wish you were learning about sharks, Dominic?” Adria asked, her ocean blue eyes lifting to look at her older brother, who was standing a few feet behind with a bored expression on his face.&nbsp;</p><p>“No,” Dominic replied impatiently.</p><p>“Whales?”&nbsp;</p><p>“No.”&nbsp;</p><p>“<em>Otters</em>?”</p><p>“<em>No.</em>”</p><p>“Starfis-”&nbsp;</p><p>“Are you two <em>ready</em> to go?” Dominic interrupted.&nbsp;</p><p>“<em>I </em>would love to learn about whales, sharks, otters, <em>and </em>starfish, Ria,” Reagan replied as she rose to her feet and took Adria’s hand. A glare was given to Dominic, who raised his brow at her as they started to make their way out of the school.&nbsp;</p><p>“What?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Why am I the only person you’re even moderately nice to?” Reagan asked him.&nbsp;</p><p>“Well,” Dominic replied in his playfully sweet voice. Long eyelashes were batted as he pretended to pout for just a second before his smirk spread across his face again. “I <em>have </em>to be kind to my future High Priestess, right?”&nbsp;</p><p>“So strategic of you.”&nbsp;</p><p>As they stepped outside, Reagan paused only long enough to pull her sunglasses off of her head and place them over her eyes. Beside her, Adria did the same with her own sunglasses. They were bright purple and shaped like seashells. Reagan had picked them out for her over the summer when Adria broke her previous sunglasses by trying to do a cartwheel and landing on them. Even if her old ones were still in tact, Reagan had a feeling these would be her favorite.&nbsp;</p><p>As they walked along the streets leading to the Coven house, Adria prattled excitedly at her side. Reagan enjoyed the walk home, she always had. It was even more enjoyable this year as she received a play-by-play of everything that had occurred during her day in Miss Cunningham’s kindergarten classroom. Adria’s enthusiasm was infectious, and Reagan gave her her <em>full </em>attention while Dominic sulked a few feet behind them.&nbsp;</p><p>“And <em>then</em>, Garrett said that Brianna wasn’t allowed to use the swings because she was too little, but <em>I </em>said that Brianna is taller than Garrett, and then he went to tell on me to Miss Cunningham.”</p><p>“That’s a silly thing to tattle on you for,” Reagan mused. “Did Miss Cunningham tell him to shut it?”</p><p>“No, she told us to <em>take turns</em>. I think taking turns is <em>stupid</em>.”&nbsp;</p><p>“I agree, Ria.”&nbsp;</p><p>Reagan gripped Adria’s hand a little tighter as they came to a crosswalk. Adria stopped beside her and looked both ways. Even then, she waited for the older Witch to step forward before she moved. Adria was always careful near crosswalks or in the park. She always looked to her parents or Reagan for guidance rather than rushing into danger.&nbsp;</p><p>“So,” Dominic nudged Reagan’s shoulder as he stepped up to her other side. Clearly, he was over his sour moments behind them. “I heard a secret about you, Rae.”&nbsp;</p><p>“About me?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Secrets aren’t <em>nice</em>,” Adria replied from her other side. “Mom says-”&nbsp;</p><p>“Well it’s a good thing no one <em>asked </em>you, now did they?” Dominic shot with a scowl to his little sister.</p><p>“<em>Dominic</em>,” Reagan gave the Warlock a sharp look before she squeezed Adria’s hand. “Your mom is <em>right</em>. My mom says that we don’t keep secrets, except for fun ones, like what you’re getting for Christmas and stuff.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Those are my <em>favorite</em>!” Adria lit up, her eyes brightening as she looked to Reagan. “This year, I hope I get-”&nbsp;</p><p>“Adria, will you shut up?” Dominic grumbled.&nbsp;</p><p>“Shut up isn’t-”&nbsp;</p><p>“How about you shut the <em>fuck </em>up<em>,</em> then?”&nbsp;</p><p>“That’s-”&nbsp;</p><p>“Dominic,” Reagan glared at her friend beside her, who was scowling in return. “Do you think, that for once, you could stop arguing with her like <em>you </em>are the one who is five and not her?”&nbsp;</p><p>Dominic crossed his arms over his chest as he looked away from her. She sighed in return. She never faulted Adria’s quips back at her brother. Adria was five years old. Dominic, on the other hand, would be seventeen in a few months. Even then, sometimes Reagan wondered which of the two siblings was the mature one. Often, she considered it to be the five year old.&nbsp;</p><p>Adria had just began to launch into a story of the songs they sang in music class when they came to the main, busy road that separated their school from the Eastern Coven Manor. Cars rushed past them as they stood near the crosswalk at high speed. If it weren’t for the light to protect them as they crossed, Reagan would have been terrified of this road.&nbsp;</p><p>“Dom, can you press the crosswalk button?” Reagan asked.&nbsp;</p><p>“Can’t you?”&nbsp;</p><p>“Seriously?”&nbsp;</p><p>The Witch sighed, letting go of Adria’s hand as she stepped the few feet to the side to press the button. Dominic was often sour around others, though rarely Reagan. Truly, she was one of the few that got his sweeter side, though it faded into sulking whenever his younger sister was around. Or rather, whenever the attention wasn’t <em>fully </em>on him. Reagan could, sometimes, understand it when it came to boring Coven meetings. When it came to a <em>child</em>, it annoyed her beyond measure.&nbsp;</p><p>“<em>Rae</em>!”&nbsp;</p><p>Adria’s screech pulled her gaze up and in the direction she had been standing. At the same time, the honking of car horns and screeching of tires filled the air. The tiny Witch was no longer safely on the sidewalk. Now, she was on her hands and knees in the street with a car coming full speed at her. On the sidewalk, Dominic looked smug.</p><p>Reagan lunched forward. Her hand flung out, finding her grip on her ability. She felt the pressure of gravity and the way it felt like strings she could manipulate. With barely a thought, she lifted the child into the air just in time for the car to fly by them. Had she reacted a <em>second </em>later, the car would have hit her.&nbsp;</p><p>“Slow <em>down, </em>asshole!” Reagan yelled, though the car was long gone.&nbsp;</p><p>Her ability tugged Adria closer until she was safely on her feet on the sidewalk. Reagan scooped the girl into her arms. Adria’s arms and legs went around her and her face was buried into the nape of Reagan’s neck. She was wailing. She held her tighter.&nbsp;</p><p>“I’ve got you. You’re okay. It’s okay, it’s okay…” She said again and again in an attempt to soothe the distraught child. Her own heart raced in her chest.&nbsp;</p><p><em>If I had been a second slower…&nbsp;</em></p><p>“Dom… Dom <em>pushed</em>-”&nbsp;</p><p><em>What?&nbsp;</em></p><p>Reagan spun on the Warlock, who put his hands up in defense. Even as he fixed her with that long eyelashed, innocent stare of his, that split second smirk she had seen as he watched Adria in the middle of the street flooded her mind.&nbsp;</p><p>“What the <em>fuck</em> is wrong with you?!” She shouted.&nbsp;</p><p>“Rae…”&nbsp;</p><p>“No, <em>fuck </em>you,” Reagan spat. No amount of his excuses would keep her from knowing that <em>he </em>had been the one to cause that. He had nearly…&nbsp;</p><p><em>If I had been a second slower…&nbsp;</em></p><p>The light for the crosswalk turned, and she stepped forward. She held Adria tight in her arms. Her gravity ability aided her in making the child feel as light as a feather. Even if it hadn’t, her rage would have pulled her forward. Adria was still wailing in her arms. Behind her, she knew Dominic was close. It didn’t matter, she could see the Coven house. Inside it was her mother or their parents, if not both. Inside, she would tell them what he had done. She would insure it <em>never </em>happened again.&nbsp;</p><p>Hands grabbed at her shirt just before she crossed the last street, and she felt herself get shoved against the brick wall of the pastry shop they often frequented. She loved this building and the elderly Were who ran it. Now, it was being used as a place to hold her tightly against as he stepped up in front of her. Her arms gripped at Adria protectively.&nbsp;</p><p>“If you tell <em>anyone</em>, Reagan, you’ll be next. I’ll make sure it’s <em>both </em>of you in the street.”&nbsp;</p><p>Fury flared in Reagan’s eyes, and she grabbed ahold of her ability once more. She didn’t have to lay a hand on him. It was a thought that shoved him backwards and to the ground. It was barely a fraction of her strength. She felt Adria’s hands tighten on her shirt. She held her even closer.&nbsp;</p><p>“If you threaten me or her again, Dominic, I’ll make sure it’s the last thing you do.”&nbsp;</p><p>“Rae-”&nbsp;</p><p>“I am your future High Priestess, and you will <em>not </em>hurt me <em>or </em>her.”&nbsp;</p><p>Dominic started to get to his feet, but she shoved him back down once more. She turned, leaving him on the ground as she quickly walked forward. She crossed the path and headed up to her home with Adria in her arms. One glance was given over her shoulder to the Warlock, who had risen to his feet, but hadn’t started after them. He was scowling.</p><p>Fear gripped at her. For the first time, she was <em>afraid </em>of Dominic, the Warlock she considered to be her closest friend.&nbsp;</p><p>Reagan set Adria down on her feet as she turned the knob to let them both into the Manor house. She looked over her shoulder to see him still in place before she stepped in and shut the door. Adria had rushed forward into the arms of her mother, who had stepped out of the office to greet them both. Inside, she could see Adria’s father, the Coven Ambassador, sitting at the desk. Her own mother, who she wanted most of all, was absent.&nbsp;</p><p>“Adria, my darling,” Abigail Benson murmured as she brushed her daughter’s hair back. “You’re crying. And bleeding-”&nbsp;</p><p>“D-” Adria started before her lip quivered and the tears overtook her again.&nbsp;</p><p>Reagan instinctively stepped forward, but she was enveloped in her mother’s arms. She was the one who would comfort the child now. Reagan, now, had another job. She lifted her blue eyes to meet the eyes of Bradley, who had made his way into the foyer to check on his sobbing, bleeding daughter.&nbsp;</p><p>“I need to talk to you,” Reagan said as she stood up a little taller. “Both of you. Immediately.”&nbsp;</p>
