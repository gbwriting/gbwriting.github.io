<blockquote data-alt="true" class="quote-alt">October 3, 2014<p><strong>Tamzin</strong></blockquote><p>“I couldn’t help but notice that you were drinking alone.”</p><p>The voice pulled Tamzin’s steel blue eyes up from the drink she had been staring at for the last ten minutes. She had been lost in thought and found that she was relieved by the interruption. Somehow she was able to muster up a small, polite smile. It didn’t seem like the woman, a Witch and a beautiful one at that, believed it. Still, she saw the flicker of relief in the woman’s intriguing blue eyes as she offered her own smile, one that was more sincere than Tamzin’s had been.</p><p>“Am I? Here I thought I was surrounded by all of my closest friends.” The Fae was surprised to find that her tone was playful, especially considering all she was feeling right now was heartbreak. Thankfully the woman chuckled at the joke, and it only partially sounded like a pity laugh.</p><p>“In that case, I am so sorry to interrupt.” The Witch smiled and took a small step closer before she extended her hand. “I’m Miranda.”</p><p>Tamzin reached out and took the offered hand in a small shake, “Tamzin. It’s nice to meet you, Miranda.”</p><p>“The pleasure is all mine. Can I join you?”</p><p>“Be my guest.” Tamzin motioned to the empty barstool beside her before she finally lifted the drink to her lips and took a sip. She couldn’t help but wonder why the woman wanted to join her, and yet she was grateful to have the company.</p><p>“So, what brings you and all your friends out here tonight?” Miranda turned so she was partially facing the Fae, she couldn’t help but wonder what caused the sadness in her eyes. Her head tilted the slightest bit, Tamzin was beautiful, and she couldn’t help but feel the urge to try and make her night better.</p><p>What did bring Tamzin out to the bar tonight? Her mind drifted to Andie, the woman she loved with every last piece of her and the engagement that ended. The ache was still there, she had a feeling it would be there forever. The last week had been hell for both her and Andie, they were still sharing the home they purchased together. It was no longer the dream they both shared, now it was a nightmarish prison that was filled with the constant reminder that they weren’t together anymore.</p><p><em>That damn vision. It ruined everything.</em></p><p><em>Did you think you would actually get a happy ending after everything?</em></p><p><em>You don’t deserve it, and Andie will be much happier without you.</em></p><p>The voice wasn’t one she recognized, but she couldn’t help but believe that what it was saying was true. Hadn’t the relationship with Andie been filled with pain and heartache for both of them? They were happy together, but then the vision came to her. It was obvious that there was no happy ending, at least not for her. Andie would be fine, she would be happy and marry someone else. It was what she saw, after all, and every vision she had before came true. Besides, she couldn’t take the happiness she saw in Andie away. Happiness that didn’t rest with her, but in the heart of someone else.</p><p>Once more, Tamzin felt that sharp pain in her chest, the one that was the constant reminder that she just lost the only person she ever loved. The only person she would ever love again. It didn’t matter what happened to her though, as long as Andie was happy. All she ever wanted was Andie’s happiness, but she hoped that it would be found with her. That had been the plan all along, and now Tamzin wasn’t sure what would happen next. They had just moved to Greenborough in June, four months ago, with the hopes of getting married and having the life they always dreamed of together. A life that was now out of Tamzin’s reach, but still possible for Andie. At least she would be happy, right?</p><p>The gentle brush over her forearm pulled her from her thoughts. A few blinks cleared her vision as she glanced around the bar. Confusion flickered in her eyes for a moment before she saw Miranda’s eyes on her. She could see the concern in the Witch’s eyes. Or was it pity? Either way, it made Tamzin’s stomach twist so she downed the rest of her drink with a few gulps in an attempt to ease the ache that wouldn’t go away. Pain-filled eyes met Miranda’s and she studied her quietly as she tried to decide if she should stay in present company or go back to the home that didn’t feel like hers anymore.</p><p>“Sorry…” Tamzin’s voice trailed as she ran her fingers through her dark hair and gave it a very light tug as if that would help the heartache.</p><p>“Don’t be. Well, Tamzin, it seems like you could use a distraction and I am wildly entertaining.”</p><p>A laugh fell from Tamzin’s lips, something she thought that she was incapable of with everything going on with Andie. Her eyes met Miranda’s again before they scanned the rest of the Witch’s body, she really was beautiful. When she lifted her gaze to meet those intriguing blue eyes again she saw the flicker that showed Miranda was more than okay with Tamzin checking her out.</p><p><em>Maybe this is exactly what I need, a distraction.</em></p><p>“I could definitely use some entertainment.” Tamzin murmured, the flirty tone clear in her voice as their eye contact lingered.</p><p>“Heartbreak?”</p><p>“Is it that obvious?”</p><p>Miranda smiled and gave a small nod, “I’m very familiar with it myself. I just found out my girlfriend of five years has been cheating on me for at least two years.” The sadness flickered through her eyes and she gave a small shake of her head and took a long drink before her attention was back on Tamzin.</p><p>“I’m sorry that happened to you. I hope she gets what’s coming to her.”</p><p>“Oh, it will. I’m a big believer in karma. What about you?”</p><p>The universe and fate has decided I’m not worthy of Andie or a happy ending.</p><p>“I was engaged until nine days ago, I had a vision of my now ex-fiancée marrying someone else. I couldn’t take the happiness I saw in her away. We’ve known each other for thirty-five years and have been in love for every single one of those years. So, I’m bloody gutted, and there is nothing I can do about it.”</p><p>Tamzin saw that same flicker in the Witch’s eyes, but this time it didn’t seem like such a bad thing. It was nice to be in the company of someone else who could understand what she was feeling. She wasn’t glad that Miranda was hurting, but it was better to hurt with someone than alone. A small shake of her head was given and she reached for the engagement ring that was on a chain around her neck instead of where it should have been. The Fae couldn’t bring herself to get rid of it, though she did offer to give it back to Andie.</p><p><em>“No, I got it for you, Tamzin. It was always for you. Do-do you want yours back? I know it was your mother’s ring.”</em></p><p><em>“No, it will always be yours.”</em></p><p>The words echoed through her mind and she felt the tears burning behind her eyes, but before she could stop them they rolled down her cheeks. Tamzin quickly looked away and wiped at her eyes and moments later the tears were stopped. She couldn’t cry, because she knew if she started she would never be able to stop. She squeezed her eyes closed and took slow breaths until she felt she had her emotions controlled enough to look back at her companion.</p><p>“That can’t have been easy.” Miranda murmured as she reached out and ran her fingers over Tamzin’s arm again.</p><p>“It was the hardest thing I’ve ever had to do. There’s no one else for me. Just Andie.” She shrugged and looked down at her empty glass as she contemplated if she wanted to get another drink.</p><p>“I could understand that. I feel the same way, though I imagine yours is more painful. Thirty-five years is a long time to love someone.”</p><p>“It’s nothing compared to eternity, but I will love her eternally too.”</p><p>The gentle trail of Miranda’s fingers over her arm sent a chill down her spine and their eyes met once more. They started at each other in a quiet, strangely comfortable, silence. Tamzin saw the Witch shift towards her and saw the way her blue eyes drifted to her lips before meeting her eyes again. She shifted forward as well and her eyes followed the same trail that Miranda’s did moments ago. Miranda closed the distance and pressed her lips to Tamzin’s in a kiss that started off slow, but without a second thought the Fae deepened it and let her fingers tangle in the Witch’s soft blond hair.</p><p>Tamzin wasn’t sure how long the kiss lasted, but when she pulled back her breathing was slightly heavy. They looked into each other’s eyes, nothing else needed to be said. Both of them needed comfort, so they were going to find it in each other.</p><p>“My place?”</p><p>Tamzin nodded, “Yes, Andie and I still live in the same house. I-I don’t want to hurt her.”</p><p>Miranda gave a smile of understanding before she slid from the barstool and extended her hand to Tamzin. Steel blue eyes met the Witch’s and she felt the battle raging inside of herself, screaming at her to not leave with someone else when Andie was home.</p><p><em>Andie isn’t mine anymore.</em></p><p><em>I need to feel something other than pain for a little while.</em></p><p>Tamzin took Miranda’s hand and slid from the barstool, the look in her eyes showed exactly how deep the ache was inside of her chest. She was willing to do almost anything to feel something other than pain, and it seemed like her companion wanted the same kind of distraction. They were supposed to be at the same bar at the same time, even if it was only to find comfort in each other for a little while. This wasn’t love, nothing even close to it. It was a physical need being met to try and soothe the ache that filled her. She knew it wouldn’t work, but she had to do something.</p><p>“Make me not feel for a little while,” Tamzin whispered.</p><p>“It would be my pleasure,” Miranda replied with a sad smile before she led Tamzin from the bar.</p><p><em>I’m sorry, Andie. I’m sorry that I wasn’t enough.</em></p><p><em>You will always have me.</em></p><p><em>Even if I can’t have you.</em></p>
