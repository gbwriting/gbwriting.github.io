<blockquote data-alt="true" class="quote-alt">February 15th, 2002<p><strong>Hendrix</strong></p></blockquote><p>The last time the mood had been as somber as it was now, Hendrix had been eleven years old and standing next to his father’s casket moments before it was lowered forever into the ground.</p><p>Despite the fact that his memories of that day were one-hundred and fifty years old, he had never forgotten a detail of that day. Even now, he could close his eyes and remember the starchy, stiff shirt that scratched at his skin and the way Rory’s hands clasped on his shoulders as he stood behind him. He couldn’t forget the ache in his stomach that accompanied the guilt he felt for not crying, even when nearly everyone else was.</p><p>Most of all, he remembered the look on his aunt’s face. She wasn’t crying. She had already shed all of her tears alone, at night, when she thought he couldn’t hear. Jo hadn’t been crying that day, but Hendrix had wished she was. Sobbing would have been less painful than the faraway, broken look on her face. All these years later, that was one thing he couldn’t forget.</p><p><em>Tomorrow.</em></p><p><em>He died a hundred and fifty two years ago tomorrow.</em></p><p><em>Did they think of that when they chose this date?</em></p><p>Hendrix squeezed his eyes closed for just a moment as he pushed away the thoughts of his father. Despite the ache he felt in every last fiber of his body, he knew this wasn’t the time to think about that. Not when he was meant to be distracting the children while everything else was prepared.</p><p>They were making every effort to make the day as normal as possible for them, but there was something in all of them that told Hendrix they knew. Even if they didn’t know exactly what would happen today, they had caught the somber way the adults moved around the space. </p><p>Hendrix opened his eyes, turning his head to look at each of the girls. Like he had been trained by his father, his aunt, and Rory, he took in every last detail of the scene. </p><p>Cordelia and Celeste were standing near the stairs that led up to the back deck of the Aldersley Manor. Their fingers were laced. Even from a distance, Hendrix could see the way they gripped each other so tightly that their knuckles were white. To Cordelia’s opposite side, Reagan stood with her arms wrapped around her mother’s middle. Her head was buried in her side, and Hendrix watched each slow stroke of Cordelia’s fingers as they raked through Reagan’s hair. </p><p>Hendrix pulled his eyes away from them and scanned the coastline of the private beach. Despite the cold, February air, Percy walked with her feet in the water as if it was the hottest day of July. Ben walked beside her with his hand in hers. At five, Percy typically leapt and danced in the water, but she took slow, deliberate steps now. Just behind them, Riley mirrored every movement Percy made while she held tight to Rory’s hand. At nearly four years old, Riley copied everything Percy did, even now. </p><p>His gaze fell to the gardens next. Saoirse paced back and forth next to the fountain with Piper in her arms. The only noise he heard was the infant’s wailing sobs. In Piper’s ten months of life, Hendrix could count on one hand the number of times she had been fussy. Like her sisters before her, she was an easy, happy baby. Today, no amount of rocking or Saoirse’s Irish lullabies would soothe the inconsolable girl. </p><p><em>Me too, Piper. </em></p><p><em>Me too. </em></p><p>Next, Hendrix looked to Eilian. His Queen was sitting on one of the patio loungers. Her head was between her knees and her fingers knotted in her blonde hair behind her head. Though her body was shaking with sobs, no noise escaped the Fae Queen. Next to her, Jo knelt and whispered words only she could hear, though Hendrix knew it would do nothing to ease her pain. </p><p>Most heartbreaking of all was the child who stood beside him. </p><p>At seven years old, Amelia Aldersley was always the life of any gathering. She would typically flutter from person to person, thing to thing, with one hand in Reagan’s and Percy’s in the other with Riley chasing behind them. In the summer, she would pick flowers from the garden that complimented everyone’s outfits to place in their hair. In the winter, when the flowers didn’t bloom, she would utilize her ability to create flowers in every hue to continue her endless mission of making everyone smile.</p><p><em>You’ll never be able to do that again, Amelia.</em></p><p><em>I’m so, so sorry. </em></p><p>The five children were rarely more than a few feet from each other, but now they all stood apart. None of them had been told what today would consist of. They had intended to give them one last normal afternoon, but that didn’t seem to matter now. None of them sought out each other. </p><p>Somehow, they knew what was coming.</p><p>They knew it would never be the same again. </p><p>Amelia’s arms were wound around his middle in the same way Reagan’s were to her mother. Her bright, amber eyes had dimmed, and they were focused entirely on Jo and Eilian. She made no attempt to go to her mothers, though Hendrix knew her well enough to know where she wanted to be most of all. She had been half a step towards them minutes before when Jackson had suddenly stormed inside just as Eilian broke down. </p><p>Now, she was frozen at his side and seeking out his comfort. As her Protector, he <em>should </em>be able to give it to her, but he couldn’t. </p><p>What was about to happen was one thing he couldn’t protect her from. </p><p>They weren’t even allowing him to be the one to take her, even though it was <em>his </em>duty as her Protector to care for her in the event her parents couldn’t. Felicity and Ezra were perfectly capable Guardians, but they weren’t her <em>Protector. </em>He was, and in a matter of hours, he would be separated from her. Or at least, he would be forced to only watch her from afar, constantly out of sight. </p><p>Worst of all, he would be forgotten. </p><p><em>“I’ll take her,” Hendrix stated as he leaned against the wall in the library months before, when these conversations first began. “Anywhere you need me to. I’ll take her, and I’ll raise her human, and I’ll take care of her.” </em></p><p><em>“I think that’s best-” Jo began. Despite the confidence in her tone, she didn’t look up from the floor.</em></p><p><em>“No,” Eilian sighed, raising a hand to silence Jo. “We’re not doing that.” </em></p><p><em>“As her Protector, I-” </em></p><p><em>One look from his Queen silenced him. He bit down on his lip as he met her bright blue eyes, which were filling with tears as she stepped forward and put her hands on his shoulders. Hendrix could feel her hands shaking with the touch. </em></p><p><em>“Hendrix, there’s a possibility that Amelia will never be able to come back.” </em></p><p><em>“I know.” </em></p><p><em>“And that would mean you would never be able to come back, too.” </em></p><p><em>Hendrix looked away from her then and found Jo’s eyes. Her expression was blank. To anyone else, it would be perfectly neutral, but Hendrix knew her well. He caught the split second of absolute panic in her eyes before it faded behind the mask she wore to hide her thoughts. </em></p><p><em>Hendrix never wanted to leave her. He had orders, both from his father and his Queen, but his duty to Amelia came first. As her Protector, she came above anything else. </em></p><p><em>“It should be me-” </em></p><p><em>“Hendrix,” Eilian stated. Her tone left no room for argument, and he ducked his head. Her hands lifted from his shoulders and rested on either side of his face as she guided him to look at her. “You’re right. It should be you. But it won’t be.” </em></p><p><em>“But-” </em></p><p><em>“Hendrix, I can’t bear to lose you, too.” </em></p><p><em>In both his Queen’s words and her softening gaze, Hendrix heard the real words she wanted to speak. His aunt couldn’t handle the loss of him. She might nod and agree to a plan that would send Hendrix away from her permanently, but she would end up the same way she had when he’d first come to live with her. She would weep, but only in the darkest, quietest moments when she was utterly alone. </em></p><p><em>And Eilian, who loved her just as much as she loved her own children, was trying to spare her from that. </em></p><p>“Hendrix?” </p><p>Amelia’s tiny voice pulled his attention from his thoughts. Despite the ache in his chest, he smiled for her. He hoped it was convincing, but the anxious look on her face told him it wasn’t working to soothe her. He gently pulled her arms from around his middle just long enough for him to kneel. Then, he pulled her into his arms. She held onto him tightly as she buried her face in his neck. He only pulled her closer and tried to memorize any detail of her that he could. </p><p>“I’ve got you,” Hendrix promised. </p><p>“I’m <em>scared</em>.” </p><p>“I know,” He murmured, holding her tighter. </p><p>“Everyone is going to <em>forget me, </em>aren’t they?” </p><p>Hendrix didn’t know how she knew. They had been so careful to not discuss what would happen today in front of her or any of the other children. They <em>would </em>forget Amelia, or at least, the majority of the city would. The adults around him would remember. Eilian’s court would know. </p><p>“Amelia-” </p><p>“Don’t <em>lie </em>to me,” Amelia replied. Her voice was firm and a perfect imitation of the tone Eilian used to command a room. In any other circumstance, Eilian would be proud of the young princess. “That’s what’s happening, isn’t it? They’re going to forget me, and send me away. To New York. Right?”</p><p>Hendrix’s eyes flickered to Jo and Eilian. They had given him orders. He knew he was meant to keep it secret and keep Amelia calm while everyone got ready for the rituals that would come next. Rituals that would do exactly what Amelia was saying. </p><p>He knew his orders. </p><p>He <em>always </em>knew his orders, and he never disobeyed. </p><p>Until now. </p><p>“Yes, Amelia,” Hendrix whispered, pulling her back enough to look into her darkening amber eyes. Even with the prophesy, they didn’t scare him. To Hendrix, they were the kindest eyes in the world. “I’m so sorry.” </p><p>“Are <em>you </em>going to forget me?” </p><p>“No. Never.” </p><p>“Okay, good,” Amelia whispered. Her voice was no louder than the wind. Her tone was far more serious than he’d ever heard. It was far beyond her age. “You have to help her remember.” </p><p>“Who?” </p><p>“Reagan. You have to help her remember me.” </p><p>“Amelia, I…” He bit his lip. “I can’t do that.” </p><p>“You <em>have </em>to.” </p><p>“Amelia…” </p><p>“You’re a knight, right? And I’m a princess?” </p><p>Hendrix couldn’t look away from her. Her expression was serious. Like Jo, it was a mask of what she was feeling, but like Eilian, Amelia couldn’t hide the panic in her eyes. Her words were important to her, and despite anything, he would do <em>anything </em>for his princess as her knight <em>and </em>her Protector. He didn’t say a word, but she knew him well enough to take his silence as confirmation. </p><p>“Then I <em>order </em>you to help her remember me.” </p><p>Hendrix met her eyes. For a moment, he considered telling her that he couldn’t do that. As a knight, he knew what his orders were. His lips parted to tell her just that, but before he could, a memory caught his attention. </p><p><em>“Aunt Lilly?” </em></p><p><em>Hendrix’s voice was quiet and full of curiosity, as it often was when he was alone with his aunt. She clutched his hand as they walked along the edge of the pond. </em></p><p><em>“Yes?” </em></p><p><em>“Aunt Jo, she’s cross with my father. She said he isn’t following orders.” </em></p><p><em>“I know. Your aunt thinks he needs to be with the Queen more. He thinks he should be here, raising you with us.” </em></p><p><em>“But he’s a knight. He has to listen to-”</em></p><p><em>“Sometimes, Hendrix, what is best for someone we love comes before everything else.” </em></p><p><em>Lilly’s voice was playful as she reached out to tap her finger against his nose. Her smile was radiant and contagious. Even with the confusion her words caused, he lit up in his own smile. Around her, it was impossible not to.</em></p><p><em>“What do you mean?”</em></p><p><em>“Sometimes, the right thing to do isn’t what we’re told to do.”</em></p><p><em>“I don’t understand-” </em></p><p><em>Lilly pulled them to a stop, and she knelt before him. Her hands went to his shoulders as she met his eyes. The sun caught them perfectly. The blue hue was endlessly bright, and only seemed to shine more as she smiled. </em></p><p><em>“It means,” She replied. “That sometimes it’s okay to not follow orders if it will make you, or someone you love, happy. That’s what your father is doing. You are happiest here, and Jo is happiest having you near.” </em></p><p><em>“But we’re supposed to be with the-”</em></p><p><em>“Hendrix, if there’s any advice I ever leave you with, it’s this: Jo and your father are so loyal to the Queen and everything they’ve worked for. It’s admirable, but there’s one thing that’s more important than honor and being a knight.” </em></p><p><em>“There is?” </em></p><p><em>“Yes, my love,” She pressed a kiss to his forehead, letting it linger there for a moment before she pulled back and met his eyes once more. “Happiness. Your happiness, and the happiness of those you love. That always comes first, even before honor.” </em></p><p>“Okay,” Hendrix murmured, coming back to the present rather than his memories. He pulled her in for another hug. To anyone looking at them, it would look like how he typically held her. “How?” </p><p>“My box.” </p><p>“Your box?” </p><p>“My <em>Treasure Island </em>box. The one you got for me a few months ago.” </p><p>“Where is it?” </p><p>“It’s hidden,” Amelia whispered, squeezing him tighter. “I can’t tell you, but she’ll know. She’ll find it. She’ll remember, and she’ll come save me. You can bring her. You can teleport her to me, and she’ll save me.”</p><p>Hendrix squeezed her. He <em>should </em>say no. He <em>should </em>tell Jo, and let her mind walk to figure out exactly where the box was hidden. He <em>should </em>do everything he could to assist in erasing evidence of Amelia for the protection of <em>everyone</em>. </p><p><em>“Sometimes, Hendrix, what is best for someone we love comes before everything else. Sometimes, the right thing to do isn’t what we’re told to do.” </em></p><p>His aunt’s words echoed in his mind once more. Hendrix pulled her back once more and met her darkening amber eyes. He knew his orders, and he had never disobeyed them before, but what was best for Amelia wasn’t what was best for everyone else. </p><p>“Okay, Amelia,” Hendrix whispered. “I promise, I’ll help her.” </p>
